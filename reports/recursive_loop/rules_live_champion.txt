1. Decide the day's side from the picture before entering, and define it mechanically: if the current price is above the day's opening area and the last completed leg was UP, the day's side is long; if below and the last completed leg was DOWN, the side is short. Trade only that side until the rule below forces a flip.

2. Flip the side only when a leg against your side exceeds the size of the largest leg in your direction so far that day. Nothing smaller changes your side — not a pullback, not a long stall, not a round number, not your own losing trade.

3. Never take a trade against the side you have declared. Against-the-leg trades were the single largest identified cost of the week (−$282 and −$1,056 on the two days they appeared, including a day that lost over a thousand dollars in two trades).

4. Enter on continuation, never on a turn call: with the side long, enter only after price makes a new high of the current leg and pulls back without breaking the leg's prior low; mirrored for short. If the pullback breaks that low, there is no trade — wait for the next new extreme.

5. Expect to enter many times. Target 8–15 entries per session and re-enter the same side after every exit, win or lose, as long as rule 4 presents a setup. Four to seven trades a day was not selectivity, it was absence: the two worst days of the week took 2 and 7 trades, and the flat day took none.

6. A session with no trade is a failure, not a saved loss. If the picture shows any leg in progress and you have no position and no entry for over an hour, take the next rule-4 setup on the day's side at reduced size rather than keep waiting.

7. Hold each entry until price makes a lower low than the pullback you entered from (mirrored for short). Do not exit on a fixed point gain; capturing 1–7% of a leg repeatedly is how a losing day is built, and every trade of the week captured under half its leg.

8. Cut immediately — no second chance, no averaging, no widening — if price breaks the extreme that defined the setup in rule 4. That break is the only acceptable loss; a loss from anything else means a rule was broken.

9. Stop trading for the day when realised P&L reaches a loss of roughly three average winning trades, or after three consecutive losses on the declared side. Three consecutive losses on one side means the side is wrong and the day is unreadable; do not switch sides to make it back.

10. In the final part of the session, take only setups that are in the direction of the day's largest completed leg, and close everything before the session ends. Late counter-side entries and unclosed late positions produced no winners this week.
