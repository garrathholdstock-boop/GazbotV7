Q1 · The biggest bucket of loss had no action against it. Now it does — and the diagnosis was wrong.

It named the untested fix — bank one of the two lots at a fixed points-onside and let the other run — called it “the first thing I would test next week”, and then it never reached the plays card. So the report’s own largest diagnosed loss had nothing pointed at it.

First, the diagnosis, because it turned out to be the more important half. That 39-point figure is measured over a fifteen-minute window from entry. The median hold on those eleven signals was 92 seconds. Re-measure over the window that actually matters — the time we were in the position — and it collapses:

It is also a one-cell spike rather than a plateau — +25 is worth half and +30 is worth nothing. A shadow slate settles all of that for free and costs no Saturday. It is BUILD #18.

And note what it does NOT do. On the eleven signals that started this whole question, banking at +20 recovers −$1,058 → −$34 — but only because two of them genuinely went 20 points our way. The other nine are untouched by any in-trade rule, because we were not in the trade when the move happened. The real fix for that bucket is stop width, and after that, re-entry.

Q6 · Is the counter-trend bench priced with the generous ruler or the honest one?

The objection was sharp and it deserved a straight answer. SATURDAY #2 is quoted at n=26 / −$398.50, and Part 2.6 §3 proves the nightly’s scalp-2R ruler is $7.26 a trade more generous than the desk overall and +$25.02 a trade more generous specifically in TREND_UP — which is exactly the regime those 26 fires sit in. Why the alarm fired, and it is a proper instrument note

two_ratchet_shadow_watch.rmfe() stops measuring at the first tick that is a full ATR against the entry. It is a pre-stop excursion by design — the excursion a trade could reach before its 1-ATR stop would have fired — and it is the right statistic for the question that watch asks. It is completely the wrong statistic for “how far did this trade go”. The 11:11 trade dipped an ATR against us early, then ran 87.5 points.

And the exit reconciles to the deployed rule to within a fifth of a point. It entered at 27,670.00 and the tape printed 27,757.50 while we held it. A peak of 87.5pt at ATR 14.1 is 6.21R, past the pure-6.0 chandelier’s lock, so the trail firms from start_k 3.5 to lock_k 0.5 × ATR = 7.05 points. 27,757.50 − 7.05 = 27,750.45, and we exited at 27,750.25. That is not a mis-attributed fill; that is the exit doing exactly what it was built to do on a 6.2R runner.

The answer: no integrity problem. The allegation is withdrawn.

0 of 26. There is no measurement gap to put on the card, and grind_long’s all-time −$454.00 over 98 lots on the clean ledger stands as reported.

What there IS is an instrument note with teeth, and it is the more useful finding: the watch’s rMFE column is truncated by construction and must never be read as a trade’s excursion. Part 2 §6 and its instrument-notes table now say so, and the “not physically reachable” sentence is gone. The wider lesson is the one this desk keeps relearning: a number that arrives without a failure mode is not a measurement — and that applies to the numbers we use to accuse our own books, not just to the ones we use to promote a gate.
