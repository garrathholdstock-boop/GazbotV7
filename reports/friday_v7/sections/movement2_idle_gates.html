M2 The idle-gate lab — could the six gates we already own have caught the 60 misses?

Movement 1 froze the tape: 68 big runs on MNQ this week, and we sat out 60 of them, carrying a hindsight ceiling of $8,024 on a single lot. Before anyone invents a new gate, this movement asks the cheaper question: we already own six gates — if we had simply left all six armed all week, would any of them have been in those runs? So I took all six live tournament gates straight out of deciders.py, fired them mechanically (exactly as the desk runs them — live parameters, live ATR floors, the live 55-second absorption veto, the live 5-second exhaustion confirm and its counter-regime veto) and again ungated (the raw gate shape with every filter stripped off), in-direction only, anywhere in the ten minutes before each run ignited, and repriced every exit tick-by-tick on this week's real capture.db trade prints under each gate's deployed two-lot exit. The mechanical answer is +$15.50 on six fires across 57 testable runs — and strip the single best trade and that becomes −$94.50. Ungated it is +$379.50 on 27 fires, +$69.50 once you remove the best three. That is an HONEST NULL, and this week I can tell you the mechanism in one line with a number behind it: this week's runs ignited out of unusually quiet tape (median peak ATR in the ten minutes before ignition: 12.0 points), and the desk's two biggest magnitude floors are set at 22 and 20 points — they are calibrated to stand down in exactly the tape the runs come out of.

LEAD WITH WHAT SURVIVED

Four things came out of this alive. Three of them are small and one of them is a correction to what this section told you last week, so I want all four in front of the null rather than buried behind it.

  1. The two short FADERS were the only gates that made money, and they were perfect: 3 fires, 3 winners, +$302.50. exhaustion_short fired twice for +$192.50 and rgv_short once for +$110.00, all three mechanically, all three with both lots reaching target. Every other gate lost. That is three trades, so it is a SHADOW lead and nothing more — but it is the first time in this section that the mechanical book has had a green column at all, and it is worth saying that the fade gates fired 9–10 minutes ahead of ignition and still won, which the timing ladder says should be nearly impossible.
  2. The 08-08 grind_long ATR 10 → 22 revert is VINDICATED on run tape. With no floor at all grind fires 5 times for −$178; with the deployed ATR≥22 floor it fires once for −$120. The floor blocked four runs that were collectively losing money. This section is usually where floors get accused of blocking the good stuff; this week the floor is doing its job.
  3. Last week's headline — "grind_long's ext_hi 2.0 ceiling is the single filter standing between us and these runs" — does NOT hold on this week's tape. The ceiling was deleted on 08-08 (it now falls back to 4.0). I re-fired grind under the old cell, the new cell and a 3.0 cell on the same 23 up-runs: all three produce the identical single fire and the identical −$120. The binding clause is not the extension ceiling at all — it is "VWAP slope not up", which killed grind on 14 of its 23 eligible runs. Removing ext_hi bought us nothing here.
  4. capitulation_long's require_flip is the one live filter that cost real money on this population: +$158.50. Ungated the gate fired six times for +$116.50; mechanically it fired zero times. Three of those six were blocked by the ATR≥10 floor (and that floor saved $42 — two losers and a small winner), but the other three were blocked by require_flip, and two of them were $97 winners. Thin — three trades — and require_flip was re-derived on the whole book on 08-02 with an ordering-correct win rate, so this number must not be allowed to overturn it. It is a shadow question, not a change.

THE WHOLE SECTION IN THREE SENTENCES

Enter at the ignition bar with perfect direction and these 57 runs pay $66.60 each, 75% of them green. The money halves within thirty seconds, holds a soft shelf worth $18–$29 a run out to four minutes, falls to +$6.80 at five minutes, and is negative from seven minutes out — with strip-best-3 negative in every row past five minutes.

Now put the gates on that ladder. When they do fire, they fire a median of 6.6 minutes early mechanically and 6.7 minutes early ungated (mechanical range 3.0–9.6 minutes). That is the exact rung where the ladder crosses zero and starts paying −$18.80 a run. The six gates are not losing at these runs because their exits are wrong or their thresholds are wrong. They are firing in the one part of the window where being right about the direction is worth nothing.

One honest difference from the last time this ladder was run: the shelf out to four minutes is new. Last week the curve was through zero by two minutes; this week there is real money at two, three and four minutes early (+$28.10, +$17.70, +$21.70 a run). I do not think that is a discovery, I think it is a quieter tape with slower ignitions, and the fact that it moved this much on a one-week sample is itself the caveat — do not build anything on the exact shape of this curve. What has replicated across both weeks is the two ends: entering at the ignition bar is worth several times entering a minute before it, and entering seven minutes before it is worth less than nothing.

The verdict

HONEST NULL — and here is what it is a null about

DON'T ARM. There is no version of "leave the six gates on and let them catch the misses" that makes money this week. Mechanically it is +$15.50 on six fires — nominally green, but strip the single best trade and it is −$94.50, and it rests entirely on one Tuesday (leave 08-11 out and it is −$191.50). Ungated it is +$379.50 on 27 fires, which is +$69.50 once three trades come out and +$6.50 once 08-11 comes out. Neither is a book you would put a lot behind, and both are in-sample on a hindsight-selected population with no arming rule attached.

But the null is specific, not general, and the specific part is where next week's work is:

  • It is not an exit problem. Proven by the timing ladder: give the same exits perfect entries at the ignition bar and these 57 runs pay +$3,799 at 75% win. The exits are fine. (The one exit finding that is real is the quiet-tape clip, worth $832–$1,130 on this tape depending on entry timing.)
  • It is not a threshold problem. Proven by the ungated book: strip every filter off all six gates and 33 of 57 runs still draw no fire at all, carrying $4,167 of the $7,619 ceiling and nine of the fifteen biggest moves. And the filters we do have were, on balance, helping this week — grind's ATR floor saved $58, the 55s veto saved $50, capitulation's ATR floor saved $42; only require_flip cost anything ($158.50).
  • It is a timing-and-mechanism problem. Proven three ways: the gates fire at 0.44–1.21× chance in the pre-run windows once you have enough signal to measure, four of five below chance; when they do fire it is a median of 6.6–6.7 minutes early, where the blind ladder pays −$18.80 a run; and the tape they would have to fire in has a median peak ATR of 12 points against floors of 20 and 22. Our six gates are three continuation gates that need the move to have already happened and three fade gates that need a spent move to sell into. A big run is neither of those things until it has started.
  • The one green thread worth pulling. The three mechanical winners were all SHORT FADES, all with both lots reaching target, two of them fired 9–10 minutes early. Fades do not care about timing the way momentum does, because they are aimed at a price rather than at a moment. That is three trades and I will not dress it up — but it is the only positive mechanism in the section and it points somewhere.

THE ONE STONE STILL UNTURNED

Everything in this movement tested entries before ignition, because that is what "could we have caught it" normally means. The decay ladder says the highest-paying rung by a distance is the one at zero (+$66.60 a run, 75% green) and that the money is still real at thirty seconds (+$29.40) and holds a shelf out to four minutes. We have never built a gate whose job is to arm on the first thirty seconds of a move that has already started, on 5-second bars, with no attempt to predict anything.

Two things this week added to that. First, the three winners here were all fades, and fades were the only entries whose money survived being early — because a fade aims at a price, not at a moment. Second, exhaustion_short handed us a ready-made ignition detector by accident: it refused ten runs because the aggressive flow WAS moving price. Inverted, that clause is a twenty-second "a real move is underway" test that already runs on this desk's tape, costs nothing, and fires exactly where the ladder says the money is. Combine that with the US-session skew (+$41.00 a run vs +$10.10 overnight) and you have the shape of the thing Movement 3 should be trying to build.

Honest scope on all of it: one week, five trading days, 57 runs, in-sample, hindsight-selected, on the quietest tape of the summer. Nothing in this section is a Saturday deploy.
