FINDING 2 — THE MGC DAY RIDER, AND WHY A PLATEAU IS THE EVIDENCE

This is the best-shaped thing gold has produced. The rule is simple enough to say in one line: at 13:30 UTC start watching; an hour in, ask the live drift detector which way the day has efficiently already moved; if it is confirmed, take two lots that way and hold to the 20:40 flat. One trade a day, never overnight, and it finishes inside the session you watch.

The thresholds were derived from gold, not borrowed from MNQ. You asked for that specifically. The method was to find what percentile MNQ's live constants occupy in MNQ's own distribution, then read gold's number at the same percentile — transferring the selectivity, not the number. Tonight's derivation:

MNQ's live ER floor of 0.15 sits at MNQ's 65th percentile; its roundtrip floor of 0.45 sits at the 53rd. Read off gold's own distribution those percentiles give ER 0.155 and RT 0.417 — within a whisker of MNQ's numbers. Gold's ATR is ten times smaller and the thresholds barely move, because efficiency and roundtrip are ratios and the size cancels out. That had to be measured rather than assumed, and it is the answer to "why are you using MNQ thresholds": we are not — they simply landed in the same place.

PART B — THE STANDING GRIND LEAD: which enable-mechanism actually works?

The question on the table: what best captures grind's trend-day upside while killing its chop-churn — (a) trend-day-only arming, (b) its own entry trend-confirmation, or (c) the current ER-0.35 floor? I built the book to answer it properly: every grind_long signal on 26 days of tick-covered tape, fired exactly as the live gate fires it (slope 0.4, fast-slope, ATR ≥ 22 — the floor that is genuinely deployed), then exited tick by tick as the live managed slate exits: Lot A a 2.5R scalp, Lot B the loose-then-lock chandelier (3.5 → 6.0R → 0.5), both behind the native 1-ATR stop, $1.50 a round trip per lot. FIRST, A CORRECTION: OPTION (c) DOES NOT EXIST

There is no ER-0.35 floor on grind. deciders.ER_FLOOR is an empty dictionary. The floors were deleted on 1 August, and the audit note in the code says they had not blocked a single live trade since 29 July anyway, because the dual-slot cutover broke the lookup. When they were re-derived on the blocked book, grind's ER ≥ 0.35 turned out to have been blocking a profitable population (+$2,923 over 173 signals). So the brief's option (c) is a ghost — and tonight's book says deleting it was right, which I will show in a moment.

What actually decides whether grind trades is two things: the ATR ≥ 22 floor inside the gate, and the on/off switch outside it. The 0.35 number does still exist — but in open_hour_watch.py, as one leg of the break trio that is allowed to arm a benched momentum gate: last-hour ER ≥ 0.35 AND last-hour ATR ≥ 18 AND a new session extreme is taken. So the real question (c) is "is the break trio the right arming test", and that is answerable.

(c) is dead, and I will name the tests. At ER ≥ 0.35 it throws away nine of the ten best trades in the book — that is the fake-filter rule failing outright. Its strip-3 is −$1,044, meaning what is left after removing three trades is a loser. This independently confirms the 1 August decision to delete the floor, on a different population and a different exit model.

(b) is worse than dead — it is backwards. Requiring a 15-minute impulse our way takes +$2,407 down to +$198 and 391 of 400 random subsets beat it. The reason is visible in the trades themselves: the ten worst fires had 15-minute impulses of +$113, +$139, +$147, +$192 and +$263 points, which are bigger than the winners'. (a) is directionally real but noisy. Requiring the session to have shown some efficiency improves things at 0.02 and 0.05 (+$2,780 and +$2,630 against the base's +$2,407, on 96 and 45 trades of the 133), but the curve is not monotone — 0.04 dips to +$1,944, 0.06 falls to +$1,204, 0.08 collapses to +$10. That is not a plateau, it is a wobble. And the 0.05 cell keeps only 3 of the 10 best trades, which is the fake filter tell again.
