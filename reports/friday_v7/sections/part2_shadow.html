THE OVER-TRADING CAVEAT, BAKED IN

The shadow board overstates and always will. A sim takes every signal its rule generates with no position limit, no benching, no router, no slippage beyond the repricing, and no operator watching. The live desk takes a fraction of them. thrust_loose fired 611 times in four weeks; the live tournament took 58 signals in the whole of this week. So the board nominates — it tells you which mechanism is worth a live slot — and the paper desk judges. A shadow sim's dollar total is never the dollar total you would have banked, and section 3 below is a $2,349 demonstration of exactly that.

THE DIAGNOSIS

The live short side runs a wider profit ladder than the long side — from data/exit_overrides.json, abs_veto_short is A 1.5R / B 2.5R while abs_veto_long is A 1.0R / B 1.5R. A wider target does not widen the stop, but it does change which trades reach it: positions that would have banked at 1.0R are still open at 1.5R, and a chunk of them travel all the way back to the stop instead. That converts winners into full-size losers, which is precisely the signature in the table — same winner size, more of them turning into losers, bigger average loss. The short side's entry is the best thing on the shadow board. Its exit ladder is the widest on the live desk. Those two facts do not belong together.

Normalising the software failures first, per the standing rehab rule

Before blaming strategy, strip the malfunctions. Two STOP_UNFILLED legs (−$106) and two MAX_HOLD legs (−$255.50) are handling faults, not the gate's opinion about the market:

One hypothesis I tested and rejected. The abs_veto_short ATR ≥ 16 floor was deleted on 1 August, and the live short's bleed does start right after: +$13.80 a leg before (26 legs, 4 days) against −$23.80 a leg after (37 legs, 9 days). Tempting. But the shadow counterfactual does not support it — since 1 August the entries that floor would have blocked (ATR < 16) are positive in shadow: +$233.50 over 38 trades. The deleted floor let in a weaker population, not a losing one. So the floor is a dilution story at most, and the exit ladder remains the better explanation. Restoring the floor is not my recommendation.

Escaping n=5 — the replay

Five days cannot test a day-classifier. But the Open Rider is a pure clock rider: no signal, no gate. Every N minutes between 13:00 and 14:45 UTC it goes with the sign of the last 15 minutes, stop at 2.0×/3.0×ATR, target coupled at 2R, 45-minute cap. That is fully deterministic, so I replayed it over every day of tape we hold — 41 trading days, 19 June to 14 August, on 5-second bars from the V5 archive + lake + hot union (scripts/odr_day_classifier.py). That turns 5 day-labels into 41.

THE SEARCH-AWARE PLACEBO — and it kills the classifier

The correct control is to run the entire search against shuffled day labels and ask how often the hunt finds something this good on pure noise. I did that 2,000 times (scripts/odr_day_placebo2.py):

The real search is beaten by 40.0% of pure-noise searches. It sits almost exactly on the median of what random data produces. There is no day-classifier here. Not a weak one — none.

Why this was never provable this week anyway — the power limit

The day-to-day standard deviation of Open Rider P&L across the 41 replayed days is $1,384. Against that noise:

To detect an edge of…Days needed (~80% power)Weeks
$300 a day16733
$500 a day6012
$800 a day235

The forward book has five days. That is 8% of what a $500-a-day effect needs. So the deliverable here was always going to be the method plus what would prove it — not a shipped rule — and I want to be clear that the negative result above is a genuine negative on 41 days, not a shrug about five.

What WOULD prove it

  1. Pre-register one feature, not eight. The search is what killed this. Pick the single most mechanically plausible one — my vote is ATR-14 at 13:00, the only feature with both a positive rank correlation and a physical story (a dead tape cannot pay a momentum rider) — fix the threshold at 9.5pt now, and let it run forward untouched. One pre-registered rule needs no multiple-comparison discount.
  2. Give it 60 sessions. Twelve weeks at the current trade rate. That is the honest cost of an answer at the effect size that would matter.
  3. Judge it on the days it removes, not the total. Log, per skipped day, what the rider would have made. A day-filter that removes eight days and misses two of the best is a bad filter even if the total goes up.
