M1 The biggest runs on MGC this week — did we show up?

Forget what the desk did this week — start from the raw tape. MGC printed 74 runs of 1.5×ATR or bigger in the last seven days (a 15-minute move of at least 10 points, against a typical 15-min range of 7). That is the whole tape, not a top-ten. ★ REV2 — gold gets its own words. The MNQ template produced nonsense here and it has been rewritten. We caught 0, we fought 0 and we sat out all 74, and there is nothing surprising in that: the desk has never placed a single trade in gold. Not one MNQ gate is even eligible on MGC — they are wired to the Nasdaq slate, and the scope is explicit that a gold gate must be invented fresh rather than ported. So 100% of the $10,649 single-lot ceiling is on the table by construction, and any sentence about “what the fading gave back” is meaningless, because there was no fading, no catching and no position of any kind.

The honest question this census can answer is a different one: which of these 74 runs would the gold candidates in Part 2.5 Part A have been in? That is where the MGC day rider and the coil bouncer earn or fail to earn their shadow slates (BUILD #5), and it is the only way this table becomes a measurement rather than a list. Note the multiplier while reading every dollar below: MGC is $10.00 a point, five times MNQ — a 12-point gold run is $120 a lot, not $24.

What the book column says now that it exists. Across all 74 runs the far-side depth share has a median of 0.505 — dead centre — and the side price ran toward was thin (below 0.50) before 30 of 74 runs, 41%. Split by direction it is 16 of 36 up-runs and 14 of 38 down-runs. The gold book does not telegraph a run at 250 ms resolution; it is a coin. That is the same answer Movement 3 reaches from the other end and much harder: on 397 book breaks, “the far side is empty” loses −$2,059.50 at the 4.8th percentile of its own placebo, i.e. the feature carries real information and the sign is inverted. A gold level that breaks into a vacuum is one nobody is defending, and price comes straight back. Read this column as a null with a hint of an inversion, and read Movement 3 for the test that priced it.

The next two movements ask whether we can do better — first with the gates we own, then with new ones.
