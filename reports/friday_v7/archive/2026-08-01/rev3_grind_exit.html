REV3 grind_long's exit, swept properly — and what the cell should be

PATCH MARKER — this section closes the hole REV2 · Q1 flagged in itself ("grind_long's 2.5/wide pair was not put through this ladder … that sweep is the obvious next job and its absence is a hole in this section, not a silence"). It also patches PART 2 · LAB's grind rows and refutes REV2 · Q0 item M5 (grind_long → {a_r 0.5, b 1.0}), and it extends REV2 · Q3 by grading its pre-registered freeze cell on tick data a fortnight early. Where this section and any of those disagree, this section is the correction.

Garrath — the short version is that we were arguing about the wrong knob. I swept grind_long's exit across 130 configurations on 3,064 tick-repriced entries over 16 sessions, and not one exit setting makes the gate green. The loaded default (Lot A 2.5R + Lot B wide lock-chandelier) loses −$2,115 over those 16 sessions; the single best cell on the board still loses −$414. What the sweep does settle is the direction of travel, and it is the opposite of what the Friday report recommended: tighter is worse, not better. The M5 proposal (0.5R / 1.0R) is −$1,127 worse than what is loaded today and fails every robustness test I ran. My recommendation is the small, pre-registered move: write {"grind_long": {"a_r": 3.0, "b": "wide"}} — Lot A from 2.5R to 3.0R, Lot B unchanged — worth +$617 over the 16 sessions against the loaded baseline. And then stop tuning the exit, because the same data says the arming decision is worth three to five times as much.

The one-paragraph answer

Recommended cell: {"grind_long": {"a_r": 3.0, "b": "wide"}}. Baseline: the built-in BIG-RUN default it inherits today, Lot A 2.5R + Lot B wide lock-chandelier, which scores −$2,115 over 16 tick-covered sessions (−$1.72 per lot-fill, 26% win). The recommended cell scores −$1,498 (−$1.31 per lot-fill) — a delta of +$617, about +$39 a session at sim fill-density, nearer +$10 a session at the live desk's actual fill rate. It passes leave-one-day-out (worst-case diff still +$222), beats the default on 10 of 16 sessions, and sits on the correct side of the one finding that survives every test: the 3.5R rung beats both of the loaded legs in both halves of the tape (+$997 archive, +$705 capture). It fails the bootstrap bar (P(diff>0) = 0.82, not 0.95), fails strip-the-best-3-days (−$130), and fails the out-of-sample sign test (archive half −$76 against capture half +$693). So it is a sensible small move on a proven direction, not a proven number — which is exactly why I am recommending the cell REV2·Q3 already pre-registered for Monday rather than the cell with the prettiest statistics (3.5R/3.5R, +$1,702, passes everything except the plateau test, and it is a one-rung spike). The single biggest caveat: none of this makes grind_long profitable. It is red at every setting, before any slippage.

The plateau test, stated plainly

There is no plateau at the winner. 3.5R beats its neighbours by $353 and $709 on a 16-session sample where session-to-session swings are ±$1,000 — that is inside the noise, and the desk's own rule is that a spike is not a result. What is a plateau is the region: on the full tape every fixed target from 3.0R to 6.0R sits between −$207 and −$985, every target at 2.5R or tighter sits between −$1,000 and −$1,915, and all three tightening chandeliers sit below −$1,900. The shape is proven; the point is not.

And the shape is weaker than that sounds, so here is the half-split before anyone over-reads it. The wide-beats-tight ordering does not hold rung-by-rung inside each half: in the 10 archive sessions the least-bad single lot is actually 0.5R (−$1,227, against −$1,264 for 3.5R and −$1,901 for the wide chandelier), and in the 6 capture sessions 1.5R (+$1,071) edges 3.5R (+$1,058). What survives the split is narrower and is the thing I am relying on: 3.5R beats both of the currently-loaded legs — 2.5R and the wide chandelier — in the archive half and in the capture half. Everything wider than that (4R, 5R, 6R) is worse than 3.5R in both halves too. So "go a bit wider on Lot A, leave Lot B alone" is supported on both halves; "wide is always better than tight" is not, and I am not claiming it.

The awkward implication for the ER floor

The 07-30 diagnosis was right — the 0.20–0.35 band is the bleed. But the fix that was shipped is a floor at 0.35, and on this tape a floor at 0.35 would delete the only profitable population (ER < 0.20, +$2,233) while keeping a band that is red on 65 fills. A band-exclusion (skip 0.20–0.35) is what the data supports; a floor is not. Filtering the sim to ER ≥ 0.35 leaves 71 fills worth −$1,035; filtering to both designed floors (ER ≥ 0.35 and ATR ≥ 24) leaves 15 fills in 16 sessions, worth −$246 — the floors as written do not sharpen the gate, they silence it. Flagging, not actioning: that is an arming decision and it belongs to you and the router, not to exit_overrides.json.

Both things are true and they point the same way. In the sim the tail is enormous — 85% of the wide lot's gross profit comes from exits beyond 3.5R, so capping there gives up something real. 1. Change the cell, but change it small. Write {"grind_long": {"a_r": 3.0, "b": "wide"}} — Lot A 2.5R→3.0R, Lot B untouched. Worth +$617 over 16 sessions against the loaded default's −$2,115. 3. The RIDE rung is not the question it was made out to be. 4. The real money is in arming, and it is not proven yet. Keeping grind_long out of the ER 0.20–0.35 / "building" tape is worth +$3,100 to +$4,800 on the same 16 sessions — 5× the exit lever — but it is green in the capture half and red in the archive half, so it is a direction with a strong prior, not a measurement. 5. And the honest floor: grind_long is red at every exit setting on this tape. The best of 130 configurations still loses $414 over 16 sessions, before a penny of slippage. No exit cell rescues this gate. Choosing the cell is damage control while the arming question gets answered.

Method & limits — read before quoting any number above

  • Every sweep number is a ceiling. Fills are at the exact stop/target/trail level with no slippage and $1.50 round-turn fees. Measured against the 40 real STOP fills in this gate's own live history, real stops filled 7% worse than the model; the desk's standing finding is that live losses run 1.1–3.1× modelled. Every dollar figure here is the best case, and the best case is negative.
  • Exits are priced on 250 ms ticks only. 15.3M archive ticks (07-05→07-17) plus 10.1M capture ticks (07-23→07-31). No exit anywhere in this section was decided on a bar. The 15 further sessions that have 5-second bars but no ticks were excluded from every number.
  • Sample. 5,874 grind_long signal-minutes on the 31-session tape; 3,064 of them on the 16 tick-covered sessions; 1,228 lot-fills for the baseline cell after the one-position-at-a-time slot logic. Regime buckets: whipsaw 452, normal-chop 393, building 266, clean up-trend 106, dead-chop 11. Anything under n ≈ 150 in this section is flagged inline and is a direction, not a measurement — that covers clean up-trend, US-open, ER ≥ 0.35 and the whole BIG-TREND rung.
  • Density. The sim takes ~77 lot-fills a session; the live gate, with router benching, took ~20. Rank configs on these numbers; discount the dollars ~3.8× before treating them as a forecast.
