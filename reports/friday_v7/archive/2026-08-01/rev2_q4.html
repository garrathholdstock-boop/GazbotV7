REV2 · Q4 The router's own estimator says it cost −$1,216 this week. Does it earn its keep or not?

Garrath — you caught a real one. Part 2.5 and Part 2.6 both print net router value −$1,216 and both conclude "earning its keep", and the defence in between ("those winners never ran; shadow overstates winners") is asserted and never sized. That is a verdict that does not follow its own numbers, and you were right to poke it. So I sized it. Two things are wrong with the −$1,216: (1) 60% of it is the dual-slot A/B double-count the report admits to but never removes — stripping it takes the week to −$440; (2) the estimator prices zero slippage on a book of 353 modelled round trips, and because the router blocks winners and losers, any execution drag at all moves the number up — breakeven is $1.25 per round trip, or 2.5 MNQ ticks. The standing memory's own floor (live losses ≥1.1× modelled) implies ~8 ticks, which puts the week at +$940.

But the honest answer is not "so it is earning its keep." It is that the blended number is the wrong statistic, because its two halves have opposite signs and each half is individually robust. Benching in CHOP earns +$1,088. Benching in TREND_UP costs −$1,380. The first is positive before any haircut and survives every leave-one-day-out fold; the second needs a 52% winner haircut to break even, which is outside any defensible band. Net-net the machine is a coin flip; the policy inside it is half-proven and half-broken. One number, stated band, below.

3 · Error 2 — the estimator prices zero slippage, and the router blocks both sides

The repricer enters at the signal's touch price and exits at the first tick that crosses 2R or the 1-ATR stop. Fees are modelled ($1.50/RT); slippage is modelled at exactly zero on both legs. Real fills are worse than the touch on entry and exit — this is the standing memory [[shadow-sim-understates-losses]] (live losses 1.1–3.1× modelled) and [[execution-cost-autopsy-stage1]] (measured stop-slippage ≈$1,200 leak).

The key mechanical point the report never makes: the router blocked 221 would-be losers and 132 would-be winners (mean modelled loss $39.12, mean modelled win $68.82). A per-round-trip execution drag δ therefore deepens the losses it saved you from and shrinks the winners it cost you. Both move the same way. So:

net(δ) = −$440 + δ × 353   →   breakeven at δ = $1.25 / round trip = 2.5 MNQ ticks

One MNQ tick = 0.25 index points = $0.50 at the desk's $2.00/pt. So the entire −$1,216 headline rests on the claim that a blocked round trip suffers less than three ticks of total slippage across entry, target and stop. 6 · Error 3 — the leakage column, which neither section reconciled

Part 2.5 prints week leakage −$1,083. Part 2.6 prints "$0 (durable days)" on the same row of the same table. Both are true in their own frame (−$456/−$398/−$229 on 27/28/29, $0 on 30/31), but the report never says so, and a reader comparing the two sections sees a contradiction. Worse, sitting in the same table as saved/missed it invites being netted in. It must not be.

  • Leakage is the only realised-money number in that whole table. saved and missed are modelled counterfactuals on trades that never ran; leakage is real losers that really fired. Do not present them side by side without saying which is which.
  • Leakage is not a router cost line. It counts managed-gate losers that fired while the gate was ON. The no-router baseline has those same losers. Subtracting it from router value would triple-count the router's failures — a third bookkeeping error on top of the two above.
  • What it is: an under-benching diagnostic. And it points the opposite way to the over-benching story both sections lead with — the week's realised evidence says the router was too loose early (−$1,083 through Tuesday) and got tighter, not that it was uniformly trigger-happy.

3. The real answer is a split verdict, and both halves ARE robust:
  • Defence — benching faders and benching into CHOP: EARNS ITS KEEP. +$1,088 (CHOP) / +$613 (faders), positive before any haircut, robust to every fold.
  • Offence — benching momentum in TREND_UP: DOES NOT. −$1,380, needing a 52% winner haircut to break even.

4. So the honest answer to "does it earn its keep" is: half of it does, decisively, and the other half is eating the proceeds. Fixing the one segment is worth +$1,615/week at zero haircut and is positive in all nine robustness folds — which makes "stop benching aligned momentum in a confirmed TREND_UP" the highest-value, best-evidenced change in this entire report, and it needs no new build: it is the rule already banked as [[us-open-dont-bench-trend-rider]], simply not being enforced.

5. Caveat that must travel with the number: one week, n=353 modelled signals, chop-dominated summer tape, MNQ-only, PAPER, and the regime tags are the router's own. The segment split is the robust finding; the ±$690 band on the headline is honest width, not false precision. Re-run after a genuine trend week before sizing anything off the TREND_UP figure.
