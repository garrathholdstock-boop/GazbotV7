REV3 The give-back study — how much did we find, and could a tighter R have kept it?

PATCH MARKER — this section opens new ground: there was no maximum-favourable-excursion or give-back study anywhere in the Friday report (the nearest prior art is the grind-exit section's fact table 10 and part 2's 4-trade runner-clip watch). It independently confirms REV3 · grind_long-exit fact table 8 — the overnight/pre-open window wants a wider exit, not a tighter one — using a different signal stream and different exit code. It extends part 2 · LAB by putting abs_veto_short's exit onto a sequential, tick-honest basis. And it corrects a standing assumption: the regime-3 adaptive exit selector is not running on the live desk. Where this section and any earlier one disagree, this one is the correction.

Garrath — you were right that we hand money back, and I can now put a number on it: in the quiet window 18% of live entries reach a real profit and still finish at or below zero, each one touching and returning about $116. That is genuinely what is happening. But when I tried to recover that money by tightening the Rs, it did not work, and the reason is a bit annoying and completely mechanical: our R is already tape-adaptive. The stop is one ATR and the target is a multiple of that stop, so when the tape goes quiet and the ATR falls from ~20–26 points to ~15–17, every dollar target on the desk shrinks by roughly a third on its own, with no code change. The proof is in the data: in R terms the quiet tape actually reaches FURTHER than the US session in R terms — median peak 1.13R against 0.66R on live fills, and 1.65R against 1.45R on the shadow book, the two measured independently. It only looks smaller in dollars because the ATR is smaller, and that part is already handled. I fitted an explicit tape-size term on three different proxies and tested it properly out of sample: it lost $1,850 and beat a plain fixed R in only 7 of 18 tests. Your literal “$15 / $30 / $50” fixed-dollar clip is worse still — $342 behind what is loaded today. The one thing that did survive everything I threw at it is the opposite of the hypothesis: abs_veto_short should go much WIDER in the US session (Lot A 3.5R + Lot B wide, +$2,063 over 16 sessions, passes every robustness test I have). There is also a smaller, weaker quiet-window tightening on that same gate (+$602) which I would take, but only as a direction.

The one-paragraph answer

The give-back is real and it is roughly $116 per round-tripper, but it is not reachable by tightening R, because R is already ATR-proportional and the quiet tape is not stingy in R terms — it is stingy in ATR. An explicit tape-adaptive R term does NOT beat the fixed R already loaded: out of sample it is −$1,850 across 18 gate × proxy × split cells, and ATR — the incumbent proxy — is the only one of the three that is not actively harmful. Realised range and RVOL are both clearly worse than ATR, which is a real finding and settles that question. My one recommendation is not a tape-adaptive R at all; it is a per-gate, per-window cell for one gate: abs_veto_short → Lot A 3.5R + Lot B wide lock-chandelier in the US session (+$2,063 over 16 sessions, +$20 per signal, 10 of 16 sessions better, LODO +$1,460, strip-best-3 +$512, bootstrap P = 0.973, archive +$780 and capture +$1,283). The quiet-window leg of the same gate wants the opposite — Lot A 1.25R / Lot B 1.5R, +$602 — and that leg fails strip-best-3 (−$407) and the bootstrap bar (P = 0.79), so it is a direction, not a number. exit_overrides.json cannot express either of these, because it is one static cell per gate with no window or regime axis. The concrete in-code change is in fact table 16. And one thing you should know regardless of any of this: the regime-3 adaptive exit selector you think is running is not running. Under the scale-out slate every one of the 16 sub-slots is built with adaptive_exit=False.

Method & limits — read before quoting any number above

  1. Every figure is a ceiling. Exact-level fills, no slippage in the headline, $1.50 per lot round trip. Real stops fill ~7% worse than the model. Live losses run 1.1–3.1× modelled. The excursions are measured on trade prints while a long sells at the bid — median spread 0.75 pt.
  2. 250 ms ticks only. 25.4M MNQ ticks over 16 desk sessions. No exit decision anywhere in this section was made on a bar. The 07-18→07-22 window has no ticks in any database and is absent, not substituted.
  3. Sample. Live 302 fills / 8 desk-days inside tick coverage; shadow 3,598 entries / 8 days; replay 1,426 signals / 16 sessions. Per-gate quiet-window live counts of 3–14 (rgv_short, exhaustion_short, rgv_long, capitulation_long) are anecdotes, and are marked as such in fact table 3. rgv_short produced 8 replay signals in the quiet window and was dropped from the policy sweep entirely.
  4. Two gates are not in the policy sweep. capitulation_long and exhaustion_short need the footprint / L1-book feed, which only covers the capture half of the tape. They appear in the give-back tables on live and shadow entries only. The exhaustion cell was swept separately in part 2 and I have not re-opened it.
  5. Sequential, one position per slot, throughout. No paired or overlapping same-signal accounting anywhere. A pair cell is the sum of two independent single-lot slots, which is how the live scale-out desk books it.
  6. Fill density. The sim fires more often than the live desk, which spends much of the week router-benched. Rank configs on these numbers; discount the dollars (~3.8×, per the grind-exit section's measurement) before treating any of them as a forecast.
  7. Entry-ATR reconstruction. Shadow entries carry the desk's own ATR. Live fills do not store one, so theirs is reconstructed from my 1-minute bars — the grind-exit section calibrated that same reconstruction at a median ratio of 1.016 against the ATR implied by real stop distances, so it is good to ~1.6%.
  8. What this section does NOT claim. It does not claim the quiet window is untradeable (the live quiet book is +$152). It does not claim tight exits are always wrong (they are right for abs_veto_short in the quiet window, and wrong for grind_long anywhere). It does not re-open the regime-rung ladder from part 2 or the 130-cell grind sweep from the grind-exit section. And it does not claim the give-back is recoverable — only that it is real and that R is not the lever that recovers it.
