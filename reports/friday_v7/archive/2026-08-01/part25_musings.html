2.5 Mid-week musings — the leads, tested hard

This is the laboratory bench for everything that came up in the desk chat this week. Nothing gets to walk in as a "finding" — every mid-week idea is re-run on the week's own data and given a plain verdict. This week the tape handed us the perfect natural experiment: two clean-ish trending sessions (Paris-29 +$638, Paris-30 +$1,172) followed by a violent chop-reversal that cost −$905 on Paris-31. That −$905 day is where most of the lessons live.

Lead with what survived. Three things came out of the week clean:
1. GRIND should not run naked. On the trend day it was the desk's top earner at +$703.5; on the chop reversal it bought the top for −$370.5. The fix that the data actually supports is an in-code continuation-veto (mirroring abs_veto's), NOT the current ER-0.35 floor (which was porous all week) and NOT a "trend-days-only" calendar rule.
2. The router's reaction-lag is NOT this week's problem. Measured, the reducible actuation-lag cost was about $0–$246 for the whole week, and it is detection-limited (the damage and the flip happen in the same 60–180 seconds). That dollar figure is too small to justify building a separate fast-bench service.
3. The exit selector earned its keep — adapting the exit width beat both fixed exits by ~$1,289 across the week.

1 · The direction-router's architecture — is the polling switch-file the best shape?

The objective, stated plainly: stop a gate from trading against a proven trend, reactively, without over-clipping the chop days the faders actually earn on. We built that as a 5-min durable tick (a headless-Claude judgment) that writes a switch file. It works — the desk went green twice this week under it. But the report's job is to ask whether a more elegant mechanism would do the same job with fewer moving parts and less lag.

Break the re-entry slice open, because it is the crux. Across the week, when a gate re-entered after a first stop inside a bench window, it made +$227.5 / +$180 / +$164 in three trend cases and lost only −$101.5 (grind, Paris-30 13:36) and −$145 (grind, Paris-31 13:42) in the two reversal cases. So the genuinely reducible actuation-lag loss for the whole week is ≈ −$246.5 — and a mechanical "bench-after-a-stop" trigger built to harvest it would also have forfeited +$571 of legitimate trend re-entries. That is a net-negative trade.

The clearest single case makes the point: on Paris-31 grind bought the rollover top and took four stops for −$284; all four fills opened within 30 seconds at 13:38 and were stopped before the 13:42 bench could ever apply. No poll and no judgment loop, however fast, saves those — the flip and the loss were simultaneous. That is detection lag, and the run-catcher NULL [[run-catcher-null-all-microstructure]] already proved the flip is not front-runnable. So we do not propose predicting it.

Architecture verdict. Keep the deliberate 5-min LLM loop for ARMING — that is the expensive, subtle judgment (is this a real break with ER climbing and vol expanding, or a delta-blip?) and it is doing that job well. Migrate the DEFENSIVE side in-code, per-gate, beginning with grind's continuation-veto. That captures the counter-trend leak the poll structurally can't, removes the thrash, and needs no new service. Do not build a separate event-driven fast-bench on this week's evidence — the lag it recovers ($0–$246, detection-limited) doesn't pay for a new moving part, and the mechanical version loses money on the re-entries. The dollar figure gates the build, and it says: no.

2 · The GRIND lead (operator-flagged, Paris-31): should grind run only on a confirmed trend?

Dead since the dual-slot cutover at 2026-07-29 16:20:49 UTC; last suppression line 07-29T13:29:04Z, zero since. Five gates affected. The _base() helper that fixes it already exists at tournament.py:86 and is applied correctly three times elsewhere — just not at lines 139 and 142.

Size it honestly: the circulating "7,734 suppressions" is a raw log-line count and the check re-fires every ~1s tick. De-duplicated it is 383 blocked signal episodes over 8 days (140 ER + 243 ATR) — about 48 a day, not thousands. Real, but 44× smaller than advertised.

And it changes the conclusion, not just the footnote. Grind did not fire 52 of 56 times below its floor because "ER filters are fake on this tape" — a claim about the market. It fired because there was no floor — a claim about our code. Those point at completely different fixes. Both ER floors were even committed ~18 hours after the bug, so neither has ever blocked a single trade in production. Full working, per-gate verdicts and the mandatory ship order: Part 3.6.

Mechanism (a) — trend-days-only — clean but unusable in real time. By calendar it separates beautifully (+$703.5 trend vs −$519.5 across the two chop days). But "trend day" is only knowable in hindsight, and even Paris-30 was 72% chop by the classifier — grind churned those chop hours and made everything in the ~18% trend windows plus the one barnstormer. A day-level switch is too coarse; the decision has to be intraday.

Mechanism (b) — grind gets its own in-code continuation-veto — the winner. The cleanest evidence is the head-to-head on the exact Paris-31 reversal that cost grind −$370.5:

Same day, same reversal, same direction — the only structural difference is that abs_veto re-requires the burst to persist for ~55 seconds before it commits, and grind doesn't. That single feature is worth a +$536 swing between the two gates on one day. A per-tick continuation-confirm on grind would also have skipped the intraday chop churn on Paris-30 (it only fires on a persisting thrust) while keeping the trend-leg winners, which the reconstruction confirms were genuine thrusts (ATR-5 of 99–156pt on the two biggest wins).

Grind verdict. Yes — grind should effectively be OFF unless a move is confirmed, but the mechanism is (b) an in-code continuation-veto, not the ER-0.35 floor (porous, disproven this week) and not a calendar trend-day rule (too coarse). Build grind a 55s-style persistence check like abs_veto's: it is regime-agnostic (works any day, per-tick, no polling lag), it removes the router thrash, and it is validated by abs_veto out-earning grind by +$536 on the one reversal that mattered. This is the same in-code guard the architecture section landed on — one build fixes both.
