M2 Could our own gates have caught the runs we sat out?

Garrath, this one nearly fooled me — so let me walk you through it honestly, because the honesty is the finding. I took the 53 runs we sat out this week (the frozen census — $14,519 of hindsight money left on the table) and fired all six of our live gates at them mechanically, ungated (no ER floor, no ATR floor, no absorption-confirm — the most generous version), in each run's own direction, anywhere in the 10 minutes before it lit up. Then I priced every exit honestly on this week's own trade ticks. The headline looked good: 21 fires, +$582, and it survives stripping the best three trades — nothing like last week's clean fluke. One gate, rgv_short, went four-for-four for +$478. But it is a mirage, and I can prove it in one number: fed only the 53 runs we already know happened, rgv_short makes +$478; fed the whole week's tape, the exact same gate makes +$1.80 a trade. The lab was quietly handing each fader only its winners. This is an honest null — just a subtler, better-disguised one than last week's.

What survived the skeptic — the one number that ends the argument

Nothing survived as a tradeable edge, and I will not dress the +$582 up as one. The durable fact is a base-rate: our turnback fader rgv_short throws 151 signals in a week, and only 6 of them (4%) are followed by a real run in its direction — while 13% go straight against it. The idle-gate lab only ever showed it the ~4 signals that preceded a sat-out down-run, so of course they printed 2R. That is survivorship, not skill: cherry-pick any early-entry fade's winners after the fact and it looks brilliant. Feed it the whole tape and it is a coin-flip that bleeds on costs. On top of that, the four biggest sat-out runs this week (+298, −276, −251, −248 — $2,146 of ceiling between them) drew ZERO fire from any gate. The money on the table belongs to triggers we do not own, which is Movement 3's job. Arming the gates we have is still a dead end — here is the proof, in full.

Verdict

HONEST NULL DON'T ARM Firing all six live gates at the money we left on the table produces a headline +$582 that dissolves the moment you stop cheating: the two faders that carry it are worth +$1.80 and +$0.40 a trade fed the real tape, their signals precede a run only 4–7% of the time, the strip-5 test flips the whole thing negative, the R-sweep has no plateau, and the four biggest runs of the week drew zero fire. The blind control proves the money and the exits are both fine — the gap is purely that we cannot see these runs coming in real time. Do not arm anything off this section. The sat-out money belongs to triggers we don't own yet, and hunting those is Movement 3.

Be honest about the caveats: this is one week, in-sample, with tiny counts at the runs (21 fires; the faders just 10). The two footprint gates were L2-blind on the 07-27 daytime runs, so their zero is partly a data gap, not only a logic one. And I'll flag the one genuinely interesting thread, because it would be dishonest to bury it: the turnback-fade mechanism is the only thing we own that shows up early on these runs at all — if we ever find a way to fire it only in that 4–7% slice (a regime read that separates "stretched-and-turning into a real run" from "stretched-and-turning into more chop"), it becomes the entry we're missing. That separation is the stone left unturned, and it is a Movement-3 hunt, not a knob on the gate we have.
