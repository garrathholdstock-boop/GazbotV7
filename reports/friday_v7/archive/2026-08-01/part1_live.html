Case 1 Thursday — the best day ever, and the router in the ring

The morning ramp is where the money was made. Note the five-hour gap after ~16:00 — the router benched the book into the roll-over and the desk simply stopped trading.

Thursday (+$1,172) is the week's headline and its best case study, because it was not a clean trend — it was an up-biased tape that topped out around lunchtime and then whipped violently for hours, and the desk still booked its best day ever by staying on the right gates through a dozen regime flips. This is the day to study when you want to know what the router is actually for.

How Thursday was won

The morning trended up and grind rode it — grind_long made +$704 across 46 trades on the day (32 of them UTC-Thursday), catching the up-legs with its chandelier tail. Then around 13:50 UTC the buy-climax printed (aggressor delta peaked, then buy intensity collapsed to a third), the tape rolled over, and the router flipped the whole book: benched the momentum longs and armed the short-side reversion faders. The result was the cleanest offensive sequence of the week — exhaustion_short banked four fade-claims in a row for +$372.5 (+$117, +$107.5, +$109.5, +$38.5) fading the roll-over. It then gave −$155 back on two later fires when the chop tried to resume up, netting +$217 on the day — but that four-in-a-row is the exhaustion edge on full display: judge this gate on its live fades, not its fixed-target shadow.

The one call that clearly saved money

At 14:51, when ER collapsed from 0.40 to 0.07 over two ticks and price rolled off the highs, the router proactively benched both momentum longs while the desk was flat — not reacting to stops, reading the regime flip ahead of them. Over the window that followed, the momentum shadows that would have kept firing lost about −$400 (thrust_aligned −$202, thrust_cont −$118, thrust_loose −$78). The desk sat flat and protected instead. That single defensive read is the clearest example of the router paying for itself: it's cheap to be wrongly benched (you miss a few trades), expensive to be wrongly armed (you churn), and this call landed on the right side of that asymmetry.

The router The direction-router trial — how it behaved

This was the week the Claude-run router stopped being a session experiment and became durable infrastructure — a headless systemd tick went live Friday that manages the desk even with no chat session running. Across the week the router changed the gate switches 59 times across 590 five-minute ticks (90% of ticks were no-change; 2 of the 59 came from the durable systemd tick, the rest from the session). It is benching-only, fully reversible, and paper — it can only block new entries, never force a trade — so its worst case is a missed opportunity, not a loss.

What the router got right, in plain terms

Strip out the drama and the router did its core job well: it kept the counter-trend faders benched almost the entire week (rgv_short fired only 3 times all week and abs_veto_short was correctly held out of the Friday chop after Wednesday's win), and it read the two big regime flips — Thursday's roll-over and Friday's reversal — closely enough to sit flat through the worst churn. Both green days (Wed +$638, Thu +$1,172) happened under the router. Its recurring weakness is the same one all week and in the ledger: over-toggling grind tick-to-tick and re-arming momentum a beat too early on elevated-but-not-clean chop. The fix is discipline, not code — arm grind once on a genuinely strong trend, leave it, and wait for ER to actually climb before believing a break.

Honest caveats — read before you trust any of this. This is one week of in-sample paper trading, and a lopsided one: two-thirds of the week's +$625 net came from a single best-ever day, and a big chunk of the green (+$1,223 across 12 trades) was booked by your own manual claims, not by the exits firing on their own — so the automated system alone did less than the headline. The per-gate reads are built on thin counts (rgv_short fired 3 times; exhaustion's new scalp trial only 11), so treat "abs_veto_short is a down-leg catcher" as a one-week observation, not a proven edge — it rests heavily on a single +$536 runner.
