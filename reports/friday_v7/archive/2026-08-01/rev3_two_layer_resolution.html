REV3 Granular visibility on a run — measured, and the answer is no

PATCH MARKER — this section answers a question no earlier section asked, so it patches nothing and refutes nothing. It extends Part 2.6 (which scores the router's arming decisions but never its timing) and extends REV2 · Q1 (which measured what the desk made hands-off but not what it left on the table by looking slowly). One earlier number is corrected in passing: the memory note router-trial-day2-findings-0730 records 07-30 as +$586; that was a mid-session snapshot and the Paris day closed at +$1,172. 07-29's +$638.5 reconciles exactly.

Garrath — your argument was a good one and it deserved a real measurement, so here it is. The lag is real and it is worse than you said: not 59 seconds but 65, and 120 seconds on the two abs_veto gates. I can prove it from our own fills — 81 of 84 abs_veto entries this week landed on second :01 or :02 of a minute, which is exactly T+65+55. But the money is not there. Across the two big trending days there were 45 (run × gate) pairs where the desk actually fired, and a properly filtered fast layer got in front of the desk on just 2 of them, worth +66 points = +$264 on two lots — about $6 a run once you count the 43 runs it never reached. On the four chop days the same machinery costs −$438. Net over six sessions: +$348 — and +$821 of that is 07-29 alone, so dropping one day flips it to −$473. The verdict is DO NOT BUILD, and the reason is the interesting part: the desk shows up a median 6.2 minutes into a run, and the thrust gate's own measurement window is 300 seconds. The 65 seconds of stale bar is at most a sixth of the delay. We were tuning the sampling rate of a signal whose lookback is the actual problem.

The one-paragraph answer

DO NOT BUILD the two-layer resolution escalation. Net +$348 across 6 Paris sessions and all 5 bar-driven gates; positive on 2 of 6 days; leave-one-day-out worst case −$473. The one cell with a genuine parameter plateau (abs_veto_long × aligned trend, 124 of 128 filter configs positive, median +$301) dies three ways: strip its best 3 fast trades and +$406 becomes −$68 (3 trades are $473 of a $408 book); its mirror side is 0 of 128 configs positive (abs_veto_short in the same regime, same mechanism, −$350) — and a real lag effect cannot know which way we are facing; and the 71 fast entries as a population are worth −$8.9, so the +$348 is not the fast layer earning, it is slot-occupancy reshuffling the slow book. The crude 60s→5s swap you ruled out is confirmed bad: −$256. The forming bar's hazard is dominated by trigger flicker, not ATR distortion: 56–61% of fast thrust episodes are never confirmed by the closed bar, the magnitude filter does not reduce that (57% at 1.0–1.15× vs 48–64% at ≥1.5×), and the only knob that does — minimum elapsed seconds — gives back the lag it was bought to save (69.6% false at 5–10s falls to 24.5% at 45–60s, by which point you are 5 seconds ahead of the closed bar). Every figure here is a CEILING; live losses on this desk run 1.1–3.1× modelled.

And the measurement that confirms it: across those 45 run × gate pairs, the desk's first slow fire arrives a median +6.2 minutes (mean +6.8, p25 +3.2, p75 +10.7) after the run window opens. Sixty-five seconds is at most 17% of that. The lever this points at is the gate's lookback, not its clock. gate_thrust already ships a fast=True branch that triggers off net_atr_2 (a 2-bar impulse) instead of the 5-bar move — "fires ~2 min into a move to catch its START, not 5 min in at the exhaustion", in its own docstring. That is a mechanism change measurable on the existing closed-bar data, with no forming-bar hazard, no new machinery and no new failure surface. It is not in this section's scope and I have not measured it; I am naming it because it is where the 300 seconds live.

The accounting trap I nearly reported as a win

The 71 fast entries, taken as a population and scored on their own, are worth −$8.9. So the +$348 does not come from the fast layer making money. It comes from the fast entries occupying the slot at different moments, which changes which slow trades were available to take — the slow book in the two-layer run made $357 more than the same slow book run alone. That is sequencing luck, not an edge, and it is the same class of error as reading an MFE peak as a win rate. On the crude setting the effect is unmistakable: its fast fires "made" +$756 while destroying −$1,012 of the slow book underneath.

VERDICT — DO NOT BUILD, and the number behind it

Do not build the two-layer resolution escalation. The whole upside on the two best trending days we have on record is +$264 across 45 runs ($5.9 a run), it touches 5 of 92 real fills for +$96.5, the six-session net is +$348 which is −$473 with one day removed, and the best cell on the board goes to −$68 when its three best trades are stripped. Its mirror side is negative in 128 of 128 filter settings, and the fast entries as a population are worth −$8.9. Against that: new state (a forming-bar reader), a new failure surface on the desk's hottest path, and a 56–61% false-fire rate that no filter on the magnitude axis reduces.

What to do instead, in priority order. (1) If the lateness on runs is the thing that bothers you — and it should, we show up a median 6.2 minutes in — the number to attack is the gate's 300-second lookback, not its 65-second clock. gate_thrust(fast=True) already exists and triggers off the 2-bar impulse; that is a Saturday-window sweep on closed-bar data with no new machinery, no forming bar and no new failure mode. I have not measured it and I am not recommending it yet — I am saying that is where the 300 seconds are. (2) Note what the desk already knows: abs_veto's 55-second wait is half its total lag and it is the reliable momentum gate precisely because it waits. Speed and edge are not the same axis here. (3) The one thing in this study worth keeping is not the fast layer but the escalation gate — ATR expanding + the slow gate nearly ready. On its own it labels a state the router currently reads by hand, and Fact table 9 shows it, not granularity, is carrying every positive number in this section. That is worth a look as a router input, benching-only and fully reversible, which is a different and much cheaper build.

Honesty banner — the limits of this section

Six Paris sessions (07-27, 07-28, 07-29, 07-30, 07-31, 08-03), one summer regime, MNQ only, PAPER. Two of the six are trend days, which is the fair test but also means the trend evidence rests on n=2 days. Every cell in the matrix is thin: 0 to 34 fast fires over six sessions, and the WHIPSAW and TREND_COUNTER columns (n=6 and n=2) should be read as no evidence rather than as evidence. Every P&L figure is a CEILING — the desk's own standing finding is that live losses run 1.1–3.1× modelled, and the 250ms tie rule is pessimistic but the 250ms resolution itself still hides sub-bucket paths. Declared substitutions: grind's Lot B wide lock-chandelier and rgv's tight chandelier are modelled as fixed R (identical in both arms, so the delta stays pure entry-timing, but their absolute levels are not the live exits); the modelled slow baseline is not the live desk (the router benched gates intraday and the footprint gates are excluded), so the slow column is a like-for-like control, not a reproduction. What would change the verdict: a real trend week rather than two days, with the fast-fire n per cell above ~50, and side symmetry holding. Until then the asymmetry between abs_veto_long and abs_veto_short is the single most likely explanation of everything positive here.
