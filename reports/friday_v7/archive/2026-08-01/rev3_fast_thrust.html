REV3 The 2-bar thrust window — it does not arrive earlier, and it earns less

The one-paragraph answer

DO NOT BUILD gate_thrust(fast=True), in any of the five forms I tested. Fast-always is +$274 over six Paris sessions on the two live thrust gates — positive on 2 of 6 days, LODO-worst −$847, and −$371 once the two trades the slow arm never takes are removed. The short side is negative at all six thresholds swept (1.0 to 2.5) while the long side is non-monotone with no plateau, which is the same mirror failure that killed the previous section. And the decisive test: scored as unblocked signal populations, net_atr_2 is worth −$5.67 a trade against net_atr_5's −$4.09 (n=208 vs n=299) — the fast trigger is not a better-timed version of the slow one, it is a different and sparser one that selects worse trades. The premise the whole idea rested on was that a 120-second window must notice a move sooner than a 300-second one. VERDICT — DO NOT BUILD, and the number behind it

Do not ship gate_thrust(fast=True) on abs_veto_long or abs_veto_short, and do not ship the trend-gated version. The mechanism does not do the thing it was proposed to do: paired run by run on the two trend days it arrives a median 0.00 minutes earlier and a mean 0.56 minutes later, at a price 3.66 points worse, worth −$8.0 per run; 86% of slow triggers have no earlier fast signal at all; it reaches 41 of 86 big runs against the slow window's 54. The money agrees: +$274 over six sessions, 2 of 6 days green, −$847 leave-one-day-out, −$371 stripping two trades, and as a signal population it is −$5.67 a trade against −$4.09. The trend-gated combination is −$1,164 and is −$1,366 in the very cell it targets.

What to take away instead, in priority order. (1) Stop attacking arrival time by shortening lookbacks. Two measurements now say the lookback is not what sets arrival: the window swap buys zero minutes, and grind_long's 720-second window arrives earlier than thrust's 300-second one. The remaining candidates are the confirmation wait and the bar boundary, and Fact table 13 shows the wait is not safely removable. (2) Accept that the desk is late on purpose. Between this section and the last, the two things that would genuinely get us in sooner — reading the forming bar, and dropping the absorption veto — are respectively a new failure surface with a 56–61% false-fire rate, and the deletion of our single most valuable momentum filter. Lateness is what those two are buying. (3) The measurement worth keeping is the arrival census itself. It is cheap, it runs off the existing checkpoint cache, and it now has a baseline: +7.58 min median, 30 of 46 trend-day runs reached. If any future entry change claims to get us in sooner, this is the instrument that will tell you in an afternoon whether it does — before anyone measures its P&L and gets fooled by one Wednesday.
