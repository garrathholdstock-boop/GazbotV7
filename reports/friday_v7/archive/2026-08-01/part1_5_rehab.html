1.5 Live-Desk Rehabilitation — never bench a gate at face value

Garrath — this is the section you care about most, so here is the deal up front. Nothing on this desk gets binned because it had a red week. Every red gate and every leaky exit got walked through the full six steps — what looked broken, what would the loss have been if the plumbing worked, is it the base / the exit / the signal, what fixes were tried (and the winners-kept test each one had to pass), does it survive stripping the best days and dropping any single day, and finally a verdict. This week five targets went through the wash. Three came out fixed, one is a chop-only specialist, one goes back to the bench to earn its keep in shadow, and nothing earned a retirement. Then — because the honest failures are the whole point — every dead end from this week's attempts is laid out in the graveyard at the bottom. We earn your trust by showing you the graves, not by hiding them.

The one rule behind everything below
A fix that "wins" only by throwing away the big tail winners is a fake win and gets rejected on sight. Every survivor here had to keep the winners and cut the bleed. Every number is from a real tick-honest replay ($2/pt, $1.50 a round-turn per contract) on this week's tape plus, wherever possible, a second different regime — never a made-up figure, never a blanket cross-tape average (that averages the days a gate should be on with the days it should be off, and lies).

And it changes the conclusion, not just the footnote. Grind did not fire 52 of 56 times below its floor because "ER filters are fake on this tape" — a claim about the market. Both ER floors were even committed ~18 hours after the bug, so neither has ever blocked a single trade in production. Full working, per-gate verdicts and the mandatory ship order: Part 3.6.

Two floors are configured against this gate — and neither has executed since 07-29, so they cannot have destroyed anything this week; as written they are backwards and too tight, which is exactly why they must not be re-wired at their current values. The ATR ≥ 24 floor is far too tight for this regime. Swap both for a single "don't buy when already stretched" ceiling (ext_atr ≤ 2.0) and the gate flips right over:

Grind is being made less bad, not good — which is why the play card for this now carries £0, not £700.

3. chandelier runner give-back — FIXED per regime

This is the desk's biggest gross winner (+$4,774 on the CHANDELIER exit) but it leaks the tail — across all managed-profit exits the desk banks only 47% of peak, leaving ~$8,088 on the table. Your symptom trade reproduces exactly: 07-27 grind runner, peak 4.20R / $208, banked +$23 — gave back $185.

The cause is one knob used everywhere: the firm lock at lock_r=6.0. But the typical MNQ runner peaks at ~4.5R — below 6.0 — so on most runs the lock never engages and the position rides the wide trail all the way back down. The fix is to make the lock regime-aware:

On the actually-taken live trades this regime policy reconstructs +$3,580 vs the incumbent's +$2,932 (+22%) — the entire gain in chop/build, trend left completely alone. It drops zero entries; it just banks the tail earlier off-trend while keeping every trend monster on the wide trail. The machinery to deliver it already exists — it just isn't wired into the one regime-blind path (the dual-slot Lot-B "wide" tail on grind_long). Caveat: the trend leg is thin (n=4 live), so re-measure after the next clean-trend week; the build fix is the sturdier leg (robust 4/4 weeks + out-of-sample).
