Why it works — and the one lever to sharpen it

The entry forensic separated 78 stamped pullback entries into winners and losers and found a genuine signal: winners fire on LOWER relative volume (median rvol 0.19) than losers (0.27), Cohen's d −0.40. The reading is intuitive — a pullback on light volume is healthy profit-taking within a live trend (winners also carry a slightly positive net_atr); a pullback on heavy volume is distribution or a falling knife (losers carry flat/negative net_atr). The losers cluster in one place: dead tape — e.g. a 20-minute MES "pullback" that fired at rvol 0.1, net_atr 0.0, sat doing nothing, and flat-clocked for −$158. There was never a move to pull back within.

The pullback prescription (🔴 lean)
Add an rvol ceiling (~0.5–0.7) and an energy floor (skip when net_atr ≈ 0 and rvol < 0.5) to FUT_PB_MAX_RVOL. It protects the desk's real edge by refusing the dead-tape and chasing-extension entries. But hold: the stamp sample is ~1.5 days — give it 5 more sessions to confirm the threshold before arming hard. Don't fiddle the core impulse/tag geometry; production beats everything tested there.

07Biggest Surprise — the desk hid a losing week from itself

Slice the gates the naive way (excluding all reconcile/reconstruct rows) and you get +$1,684. The desk actually lost −$4,443. The −$6,100 swing is real losing round-trips that the emission gap routed into RECONSTRUCTED_BACKFILL — which the gate-attribution convention drops. A high-volume chop week stressed the OrderFilledV3 emission path, so a large share of real closes only landed via the gap-tolerant reconstruct.

Why it matters: closing the emission gap at the order-state-machine level is no longer just a completeness chore — it's a P&L-legibility issue. Until it's closed, naive gate attribution will systematically under-count losses on busy days. (This report includes the reconstructs, so its numbers reconcile to the header.)
