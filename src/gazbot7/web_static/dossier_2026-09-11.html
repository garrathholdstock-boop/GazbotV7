Is there a desk here?

Ten agents, four estates, five months of work. The short answer: yes — but not the desk you think you built, and what stands between you and an automated one is plumbing, not research.

GAZBOT V7 · MNQ + MGC · PAPER · DUQ191770 · 45 trading days live · 2026-09-11

The verdict

You are on to something. It is not the thing you have been tending.

Five months produced one detector that works unattended (drift.py), one filter that makes it testable (WATCH_RT), and a measurement estate better than most funded managers run. None of those three is connected to the thing that trades.

Every component that is connected — the polled market-order exits, the manual entry button, the six-gate tournament — is where the money went. That is a wiring diagram, not a verdict on the idea.

The honest number: on the only uncontaminated sample — single-lot trades in the current architecture — the systematic desk is +$126.50 over 432 trades, t = 0.09. Not profitable. Not losing. Sitting on its own cost line, which is where real systems start rather than where they end.

01How to read the numbers

The system and its documentation were largely written by the same AI running this audit. Agents were therefore briefed to treat every document — CLAUDE.md, STATE.md, the memory files — as a claim to verify, never a fact. Where a document and the data disagreed, the data won and the disagreement became a finding.

Every figure carries its provenance:

  • re-derived  Re-computed by the lead from raw rows, or read directly from the code line. All load-bearing claims got this treatment.
  • agent  An agent computed it from primary data and showed its method; not independently reproduced.
  • qualified  True only under a stated assumption, named inline.

Three agent claims did not survive checking and are corrected in §07 rather than quietly dropped — including a false alarm about a live naked position that does not exist.

The mechanism nobody had named

The edge is entirely loss-cutting. On the 21 positions the machine would have lost, the hand saved +$10,718. On the 22 it would have won, the hand gave up −$2,663 by exiting early.

You are not top-ticking winners. You are personally acting as the stop-loss on a desk that, by your own instruction, has no stop. That is the most encodable object in trading — and it is why "claiming cannot be backtested" was true but aimed one level too low. The 119 calibrations searched for a rule to replace your hands on your trades. The finding is that your trades are the problem.

05Why the exits keep failing

The trail was switched off on 2026-09-11 on the reasoning that a polled level is not a stop. That reasoning was right and aimed at the wrong component. Reading the ladder, day_rider.py:1509 re-derived:

for _k, _tpt in enumerate(_ladder):
    if _k in done or lots_open <= 0 or ahead < _tpt: continue
    _tr = ib.placeOrder(contract, MarketOrder("SELL" if d > 0 else "BUY", 1))

The take-profit ladder is not a resting limit. It is a once-a-minute poll that fires a market order — and so was the trail. One was removed and the other left, so the desk now has less automatic protection than before. The proof is in the ledger re-derived:

id 868   LONG 1   29320.25 -> 29240.75   -$160.50   TARGET_100

The +50pt rung sat at 29370.25, and the tape printed 29396.00 forty seconds earlier. That take-profit filled 129.5 points below its own level and booked a loss.

Faster polling does not fix it — 60s to 5s measures as non-monotonic noise. What dominates is sampling phase: the same rule on the same tape swings $651 depending on which second of the minute the poll lands.

The fix is already in your codebase

LimitOrder at :1007. StopOrder at :1802, gated off by PLACE_VENUE_STOP = False. The tournament already rests stops at the broker — 312 stp-* executions in the fills table.

Four OCA-grouped limits at 50/100/200/300pt plus one protective stop, resting at IBKR. Polling disappears, sampling phase disappears, and the fabricated multi-lot exit fill disappears. One change closes three findings.

06What is genuinely good

Not consolation. Several of these were called better than funded practice, independently and unprompted, by agents that did not know each other's findings.

The measurement estate

Four independent auditors reconstructed five weeks of trading from venue fills, detected a broker-side artifact, and replayed the strategy causally — on someone else's codebase, in an afternoon. Almost no one-person desk can be audited that way. Ledger arithmetic is perfect: 879 of 879 rows internally consistent, zero exceptions.

The tick repricer

Re-run from scratch on a random 250-trade sample: 249 of 250 exact to the cent. Defensive against the IBKR sentinel, defers rather than freezes a partial window, replays each chandelier variant on its own trail.

Intellectual honesty, structurally enforced

$1,551 of fictitious profit kept in the table labelled EXCLUDE: rather than deleted. The rows the desk discards are net positive — it is biased against itself. Controls protected by name with written reasons. A pre-registration file states "an unfavourable result is the most valuable outcome this can produce". One agent: "I have seen funds that do not do this."

Killing your own headlines

The tunnel service's founding 1.75× claim was withdrawn by the people who shipped it. An agent re-derived it independently at 1.02× and found the arithmetic that manufactured the error: ATR at a break is only 0.607× an active minute's ATR, so the denominator alone produces 1/0.607 = 1.65×. The withdrawal was correct.

07Corrections to the audit's own agents

Reported because an audit that hides its own misses is not an audit.

False alarm — there is no naked short

One agent reported an open 38-lot naked position sitting for five days. It reconstructed that from the fills table — precisely what the desk's cardinal rule forbids. The venue reads venue +0 = tournament +0 + rider +0, verified twice by the independent read-only reconciler. The −38 is the missing 09-07 cover. Bookkeeping, not risk.

Inflated headline

An agent put the first-trade-of-day bucket at +$3,974.51. Direct query gives +$2,505.01 — its filter admitted phantom rows. The effect replicates strongly; the number did not.

Weakened on challenge

The V5 archive was reported as independently reproducing the negative result at −$2.53/trade, t=−2.26, n=3,008. On challenge the agent corrected itself: V5 booked zero commission, so that applies an assumed fee. Gross it is −$0.93/trade, t=−0.83. Two negative samples — not two significant ones. The direction replicates; the significance rests on a cost constant.

2 · The fail-safe is inverted. agent gate-reactivate arms every gate at 22:00Z regardless of whether the router is alive. On 2026-09-10 the router went blind at 16:30Z with all six gates off; at 22:00Z five were armed and could not be benched for 10h50m. The stated failure mode is "benched is the cheap error". The actual one is "armed and unbenchable, overnight".

3 · The shadow book has a 55-second look-ahead. agent Entry price is stamped at bar close while the decision is made up to 55s later. It flatters every delayed-confirm arm and defames every tick-triggered one. Corrected, abs_veto_55s falls from +$9.60 to +$1.17/trade and nothing survives multiple-comparison correction. The same defect has been wrongly condemning capitulation_long at −$15.63/trade when the honest figure is −$0.57.

4 · The census measures 3-hour runs and calls them 15-minute runs. re-derived run_census.py:36 sets W = 180 as a bar count commented "15-min window in 5s bars". With --lake the selector picks 1-minute bars, so W becomes 180 minutes — while the participation window stays hardcoded at start + W * 5, i.e. 15 minutes. A gate that traded minute 40 of a run scores as having sat out. That is the accounting behind the greenfield programme's founding "we sat out $8,025".

5 · The books do not tie. re-derived The fills table implies a net position of −38 against a venue of 0, because zero fills were recorded on 09-07 when the runaway short was covered. 89% of booked trades have no exec_id linkage. The largest loss in the desk's history — roughly −$8,400 — appears in no table at all.

6 · The router costs more than it can be shown to add. agent Of 4,609 gate fires it benched, 80% would have failed the gate's own ATR floor anyway — a slower, costlier copy of a one-line threshold. Measured benefit $35–350/month with a confidence interval including zero, against $352/month of inference. Its reasoning quality is genuinely high (98.4% numerical fidelity, 0.13% rulebook violations); its substrate is a consumer OAuth subscription with a combined failure MTBF of 4.8 days and a repair path requiring a human at a keyboard.

On the $30,000

Not yet — and not for the reason you would expect. The strategy is not disproven; it is untested, because the instruments measuring it were mismeasuring and the components that work were never switched on.

What disqualifies today is the risk architecture: a 4-lot naked position with no stop, a documented 444-point adverse excursion, and a weekend runaway that on a $30k account would have been a margin call rather than a drawdown.

Do Tier 0 and Tier 1, wire up D1, run the twelve sessions. Then you are deciding on evidence instead of on mood — a far better position than five months of work usually buys.

Method. Ten agents briefed independently, each instructed to treat project documentation as claims requiring verification. Databases opened read-only; no orders placed, no services restarted, no files modified during the audit. Load-bearing claims were re-derived by the lead before inclusion; three did not survive and are corrected in §07.

Sample sizes throughout are small — 45 trading days, one instrument, one volatility regime, on a paper engine that demonstrably fabricates fills. Every positive result in this report has a confidence interval that touches or nearly touches zero. That is the honest state of the evidence, and it is why the recommendation is a twelve-session test rather than a deployment.
