2 THE VERDICT, UP FRONT — before any method

Did we find a way to ride them? Yes — and it did not make money in the week you asked about

Yes. There is a single, dumb, direction-agnostic run-catcher that works on MNQ, and it survived every robustness test this desk owns. At the close of every minute it asks one question — "has the last ten minutes covered at least two ATRs in one direction?" — and if yes it takes that direction at the next bar's open, puts a wide stop three ATRs away, aims six ATRs out, gives the trade two hours, and only does any of this between 13:00 and 20:00 UTC. That is the whole gate. Over 41 sessions it makes $8,606 on 189 trades — $45.53 a trade, 51.9% wins, profit factor 1.70, about $210 a session, worst day −$460, maximum drawdown $1,037. Strip its three best trades and $6,803 of the $8,606 is still there. Remove any single week and it stays green, worst case +$6,361. On the 39 sessions before the census week it makes +$8,659. Triple the fee and it makes +$7,280.

And in the census week itself — the week with the $8,025 on the table — it made minus eleven dollars. It boarded 19 of the 60 sat-out runs and banked $1,537 on them, then gave every penny back on the other fourteen trades. Both of those sentences are true, and the second is why this is a SHADOW candidate and not a Monday deploy.

In plain daily English, here is the whole weekend in six lines:

  • Getting on the runs is not the problem, and never was. A trigger that requires nothing more than "ten minutes have covered one ATR" boards every single one of the sixty runs, a median of five minutes in, with the entire move still ahead of it. Only one run of the sixty was never boardable at all. Detection is solved.
  • The problem is that the same trigger fires 337 times a session on ordinary tape, and paying for those is what eats the money. Everything the labs did after step one is about which of those fires to refuse.
  • The strongest filter in the whole study is the clock, and it cuts straight across every cluster label. Overnight, the identical rule on the identical tape is an edge vacuum — −$197 over 603 trades, which is 603 trades of variance for nothing.
  • The desk's default exit destroys this entry. A 1×ATR stop with a 2R target is the single worst cell in the entire study at −$5,385. Median adverse excursion on these trades is 3.00 R — exactly the stop width — and the trades that ended up winning went 1.35 R against us first. A tight stop deletes the median winner before it turns.
  • Three of the four cluster labels do not survive contact with their own base rate. UNCLASS lift 0.76, FLOW-LED lift 0.84, VACUUM 51.3% against a 51.2% coin. Only the OPEN/NEWS window is real (3.31× run lift) and even there the label is just a clock with a misleading name.
  • The clean win this weekend was gold, not the runs. A 60-minute level break into a liquidity hole, faded: +$2,648 over 157 trades at $16.87 each, 22 days, five ISO weeks, all five green, beaten by zero of thirty placebo draws — and it was +$830 in the census week. It is the only thing on this page that was green in the week we sat out sixty runs.

3 THE RIDER — the pooled, direction-agnostic run-catcher

The four cluster hunts each have a sample-size problem this one does not. You cannot prove anything with eight runs and you certainly cannot with two. And a rider does not care what we called the run. So this lab threw the labels away and scored one rule against all sixty sat-out runs pooled, on 49 sessions of tape from 2026-06-19 to 08-14 — the V5 archive's 5-second bars, the Parquet lake, the hot capture for the last session, and four Sunday reopens patched in by hand.

Two things that would have produced confidently wrong numbers

The tick lake is not our biggest tape. Ticks give 28 sessions with a real hole at the V5→V7 seam. The 5-second bar stream spans 45 sessions unbroken. For a price-only run-catcher the bars are the correct source and they nearly double the sample; the ticks were then used to audit the bar engine rather than drive it.

The nightly lake mirror skips the Sunday 22:00Z reopens. Two of the sixty runs (08-09 22:02 and 08-09 23:47) live on exactly such a session. Without patching four days in from capture.db, this study would have scored 58 of 60 runs and reported it as 60. Somebody should check whether tape_mirror.py ought to be dropping Sundays at all — it is harmless here and silently wrong for any overnight study.

3.1 The exact mechanical spec

GATE      gf_rider  ("BOARD IT LATE" — no prediction of any kind)
INSTRUMENT MNQ, $2.00/point, 1 lot
CLOCK     evaluate on the CLOSE of each completed 1-minute bar
TRIGGER   |close − close[−11]| ≥ 2.0 × ATR          # ten minutes have covered two ATRs
DIRECTION sign(net) — LONG if the move was up, SHORT if down. Two-sided, cluster-blind
SESSION   13:00 ≤ utc_hour < 20:00                   # the strongest filter in the study
FLOORS    rvol   ≥ 1.0   (vs the trailing 30-minute mean)
          atr_pr ≥ 0.40  (ATR percentile rank over the trailing 6 hours)
ENTRY     market at the NEXT bar's open — a rule can never see the bar it fills on
STOP      3.0 × ATR      ★ WIDE. Median adverse excursion on these trades is 3.00 R
TARGET    6.0 × ATR      (= 2.0 R off that stop)
TIME CAP  120 minutes
EXIT      no chandelier, no trail, no break-even. Every variant tested RED — see §3.3
COSTS     $1.50 per ROUND TRIP + one MNQ tick (0.25pt) crossed each way = $2.50 all-in per trade
TIES      every ambiguity inside a 5-second bar is resolved AGAINST the trade

Everything the rule needs already exists on Features except one thing: net_atr_10, the signed ten-minute net move in ATR units. That plus a gate_board decider is the entire build cost. The five-minute version of the same rule runs today on the existing net_atr_5.

This is why every tight stop in this report dies

Half the time the tape hands you almost nothing to survive. A quarter of the time it takes three and a half ATRs off you before it pays. One run in ten takes six and a half. A 1×ATR stop is shaken out of the top half of that distribution before the move it correctly identified even starts paying. The gold run-catcher, the Open Rider stop-width work and the exhaustion_short rehab each found the same thing independently this week, from three other directions.

Step-one verdict: PASSED, decisively. The information to board a run exists, it arrives early, and it leaves a median 5.5 ATRs on the table. Whatever kills this rider, it is not detection.

The recurring desk finding, from a completely fresh angle

ER filters are FAKE. ATR floors are REAL — but only in percentile form. Both ER cuts sit near the bottom of that table, both fail their placebo, and the tighter one throws away 108 winners worth $23,448 to arrive at $4.01 a trade. That is the 15-of-17-winners rule firing exactly as intended: a filter that "wins" by dropping the target winners is a fake win, and both ER cuts are rejected on that ground. The flow-alignment cut fails the same way — requiring the aggressor tape to agree with the direction you are boarding costs 77 winners and $17,308 and lands below its own placebo.

5.1 The cost floor, and why it killed every previous gold idea

Before any gate: take a plain extension trigger (a 20/30/60-minute move of 1.5–2.5 ATR) and trade it both ways through five target/stop pairs on 1,000+ entries. Every cell, both directions, loses — −$3.60 to −$5.49 a trade. That is not a contradiction, it is arithmetic: fade and follow on the same rows are the same two trades, so they sum to roughly minus twice the round trip. When the round trip is $7.50 and one R is $16, a 1.0-ATR target pays $16 gross → +$8.50 net on a win against −$23.50 on a loss, so you need to be right 73% of the time just to break even.

That is the wall every gold gate has hit, and it is why on gold the exit matters more than the entry. The surviving gates do not take a 1-ATR target. They take a wide stop and let a snap-back run, because that is the only geometry where the payoff clears a $7.50 floor.

7.5 If we still cannot catch them — what the next attempt actually needs

Being plain about it: on the evidence of the census week alone, we still cannot catch these runs for money on MNQ. The rider net −$11, the four cluster hunts produced no run-catcher (2/16, 0/15, 9/34 for −$162), and the only gate that caught the big ones — BREAKFLOW at 11/15 — had its worst week of the sample in the very week it needed to work. That is a null on the week, and it is not being padded into optimism.

What it is not is a null on the mechanism, and the distinction matters. Eight of nine weeks say the rider works; the labs' own diagnosis of the failure is a 7.78pt median ATR — the quietest tape in 49 sessions, with a fixed $2.50 cost that did not shrink with it. So here is the ranked list of what the next attempt needs, in order:

  1. More sessions, not more parameters. Nine ISO weeks with seven green is suggestive, not decisive. The single most valuable input is eight to ten more weeks of forward shadow data, and it is free. Every candidate here has a named promotion threshold in 7.3; the shadow book will hit or miss them on its own.
  2. One instrument we do not have: an absolute, cross-session volatility normaliser. The desk's entire regime vocabulary is trailing-percentile based and therefore blind to a week that is quiet in absolute terms. That is not a tuning problem, it is a vocabulary gap, and it is the one thing that would have stood the rider down in the week it lost money.
  3. A second instrument: a volume-vs-baseline z on the router's own 1-minute grid. The only measured pre-run separation anyone found this weekend is volume (AUC 0.703 rising to 0.883 on the biggest runs). The router reads ER, ATR and structure-break — not one of them is a volume measure. It would not have saved VSURGE as an entry, but it is the only candidate arm-condition on this page with measured separation behind it, and it is cheap.
  4. A different instrument entirely, and gold is already saying so. The clean survivor this weekend is on MGC, not MNQ, and it works because gold's book is thin enough that a liquidity hole is visible — the median size resting within a point of a break level is one lot. That is a structural property MNQ does not have. If the answer on Nasdaq keeps being "the tape is too crowded to read", then the honest response is to put more research weight on the instrument where the book can still be read, and the first question there is cross-asset: an MGC break that coincides with an MNQ break is a different event from one that does not, and we capture both on the same clock and have never once looked.
  5. And a data source we do not yet capture, named honestly: nothing here needed one. Every "we need L2" recommendation was tested this weekend and closed — MNQ depth imbalance at board time is a coin flip at AUC 0.496, and the 41ms event stream says the same thing the 250ms sample does. The two genuinely untested reads are the rate of queue depletion rather than its level, and the lead-lag between a flow burst and the break it precedes. Both are computable from tape we already own.
