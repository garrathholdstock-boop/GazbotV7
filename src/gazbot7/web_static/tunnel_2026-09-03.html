Your read of the tape is right, and it is right about gold specifically: MGC spends 63% of its minutes inside a volatility compression, leaves one about six times a session, and the compressions are the same size in dollars as the index’s. That is the good half. The other half is that leaving a tunnel does not predict anything — not size, not direction — and that is true of MNQ as well. Along the way this study had to withdraw the 1.75× magnitude claim that the live MNQ alert has been printing since it was armed.

2 TWO THINGS GOLD DOES THAT THE INDEX DOES NOT

Gold’s tape has holes, and they nearly ate the model

9,156 gold minutes — 2.5% — have a true range of exactly zero. No trade printed, or one trade at the previous close. MNQ has 648 such minutes, 0.20%. On a log scale zero is not “very small”, it is minus infinity: the first fit spent an entire state on that spike and returned quiet = 0.03pt covering 2.5% of minutes. That is a detector for a dead tape, not for a compression, and it would have run green and silent forever. Those minutes are now carried as missing observations — the state propagates through them by transition alone, which is what “no information” actually means. Copying the MNQ recipe unexamined would have produced a plausible, wrong model, which is the whole reason gold got its own fit.

Gold’s absolute volatility drifts; a fixed-threshold model would rot

Fitted on raw range, gold’s quiet state measures 0.64pt in the first third of the sample, 1.72pt in the second and 1.30pt in the third — because gold itself went 3350 → 5000 → 4400 over the year. A model with 2025 thresholds would call an ordinary 2026 minute “active”. The fix is to score each minute against the tape’s own recent normal (a causal trailing median), which is what a tunnel is anyway. On that feature the same three independent thirds return quiet 0.73 / 0.77 / 0.75× median and active 1.57 / 1.62 / 1.54× — the same model three times.

A parameter that has to be re-fitted to stay true is a parameter that will one day not be re-fitted.

3 IS THE BREAK WORTH TRADING?

No. Not on gold, and — this is the uncomfortable part — not on MNQ either. Every number below races from the end of the break minute (racing from its own timestamp replays the decision bar), counts one break per tunnel, and compares against a control drawn from the same UTC hour, because gold’s activity is strongly hour-of-day driven and an all-hours average would credit the break with the London open.

The one genuine difference, and it is small

Gold’s breaks are a coin flip (0.498 / 0.535 / 0.523 against a control of ~0.498). MNQ’s are mildly adverse at the near barrier (0.444). So gold at least does not punish you for taking the break. “Does not punish” is not an edge, and the 0.535 cell is 1.6 standard errors from a coin flip and one of three cells looked at — it is noise until something forward says otherwise.

5 “TRADE BETWEEN THE TUNNELS” — THE OTHER READING

“We will trade between the tunnels” has two meanings and §3 only answers one of them. The other is trading inside the box: fade the edge back toward the middle while the tape is still compressed. It is a better-sounding idea than the break, because the compression is the part that is persistent and measurable. It was tested on the same held-out sessions, entirely causally — the tunnel’s high, low and mid are only ever “what has happened so far”, never the finished box, because the finished box’s edges are by definition where price turned around.

Over the held-out sample that is −$11,781 across 2,749 trades. MNQ is worse and loses before costs at all. Note the shape though: gold is again the less bad of the two, a pattern that now appears in three separate measurements on this page.

6 WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE DESK

Do

  • Keep watching the gold chart. The structure is real, held-out and stable, and it is the same structure you already read on the index. Nothing here argues against your eye.
  • Keep the MNQ tunnel alert running, with its honest text. It marks a state change, which you asked to see. It no longer promises a bigger hour.

Don’t

  • Don’t arm an MGC tunnel alert. There is nothing for it to tell you that the chart does not, and an alarm that fires six times a session with no edge behind it spends the channel — the desk has a standing rule that an alarm nobody can act on is an outage of the alarm channel.
  • Don’t trade the break, either direction. Coin flip on gold, mildly adverse on the index.
  • Don’t fade the edge. Zero gross, and the cost is the whole result.
What would change the answer

Everything above conditions on one thing: the tunnel. Every cell is a coin flip because the break carries no information by itself — which is exactly what eleven months of direction tests on this desk keep returning. The version of this idea that has not been tested is the one where the tunnel supplies the timing and something else supplies the direction: your own read, the session block, the VWAP stretch meter, an order-flow tell. The tunnel would then be a filter on when to look, not a signal. That is a different study and a much larger one, and it is the only version of “we will trade between the tunnels” that this evidence leaves standing.

Honest limits of this page

But production sees fewer zero-range minutes than the backfill (1.10% vs 2.95%), so the live feed is slightly richer than the one the model was fitted on.
