2 THE VERDICT, UP FRONT

No. We still cannot ride them, and I am not going to dress that up. The pooled, direction-agnostic late-boarding run-catcher was built exactly as commissioned — board late, sweep the exit wider than this desk has ever run one, filter it causally, placebo-control every cut, route it — and it passed every in-sample test we own, including two separate placebos. Then it died on the out-of-sample leg: 1,289 trades across 236 untouched sessions for a total of +$14, a 95% confidence interval on expectancy of [−0.204R, +0.246R], and a per-trade result that goes negative on one tick of slippage. It is independently corroborated by a live shadow arm this desk shipped on 2026-08-15 off last week's version of this same phase: rider_w5, quoted then at +$19.29 a trade, has taken 51 forward trades since 08-17 for −$1,471 (−$28.84 a trade), red on six of seven days and red in every ATR bucket including the candidate's own. Nobody had graded it until this weekend. All four cluster hunts returned the same answer by four different routes, and the chop-day scalper returned it by a fifth. Seventy-plus named candidates, one goes forward, and it goes to shadow as an instrumented question rather than as a candidate.

What we DID find, and it is not nothing
  1. Detection is solved. Selection is not. A five-minute boarding trigger fires inside 62 of 62 sat-out runs at a median three minutes late, with a median 41 points and 63% of the move still ahead. That directly overturns the desk's own prior (“median 16 points left, 12% capture”), which was measured with a 30-minute window — the latency was a property of the instrument, not the tape. The same trigger also fires 34 times a day on tape that goes nowhere. That gap is the entire problem.
  2. The size threshold is real and three independent labs landed on ~120 points. OPEN/NEWS: below 120pt of follow-through a correct trade still books −$4.10; above it, +$184.75. UNCLASS: runs of 130pt+ pay +$126.56 a trade while the 55–70pt band pays −$16.12. The desk's own earlier greenfield work: ≥120pt boarding pays +$69.38. Three harnesses, three clusters, one number.
  3. Four cluster labels are refuted as footprints, with measurements rather than opinions — and the census has three specific bugs to fix as a result (§7).
  4. Gold's live shadow disagreement is solved, and the fix is one line of plumbing rather than a research problem (§5).

And the one thing we now know we do not need: a new data source. The L2 book was tested as the selector across 311 fires and 264 million book rows and returned a clean null — correlation −0.009 with outcome, placebo p=0.62, no monotone band anywhere. We had the deepest data on the desk pointed at the exact question and it was empty. What the next attempt needs is named at the bottom of this page, and it is a different question asked of the same tape.

3 THE RIDER

The commission was explicit: stop trying to predict a run, board it after it has proven itself, and measure what is left. Here is that, in the order the work was done, with the kill at the end.

3.3 The exit, swept wide — and then swept again on a sample ten times bigger

The standing finding is that a tight exit gets shaken out of a grind while the same entries with a wide stop and a far target make money. That was tested properly here, entry set held fixed so every cell differs in the exit and nothing else. On 35 days of tape the wide-exit thesis is confirmed: the live-desk-shaped exit (stop 1.5, target 3.0) loses $783; widen to a 4×ATR stop and a 6–8×ATR target and the same boards make +$3,484 to +$3,495. A swing of over $4,200 on identical entries, purely on the exit.

Then it was re-run on 236 sessions instead of 28, on the banded entry set (n=1,289), and the surface fell apart:

The wide exit reliably makes the rider less negative — target 6.0 is the best column in every row — and never makes it positive.

3.4 The causal filter, and what it really does

Bucketing every board by the ATR level at entry — causal, known before the trade, the live router's own meter — and re-running a 216-cell sweep inside each bucket gave a fifteen-fold difference in survival rate between buckets. Rescored in R (P&L divided by what the trade risked) the relationship is not monotone in ATR, which kills the “it's just bigger bets” explanation. It is a band: 11 to 16 points of ATR, with both edges load-bearing. Below it the moves are too small against noise and the round trip; above it a “proven” move is more often a spent one.

The number I am obliged to report, and it is not flattering

Cutting to the ATR band discards 372 of the 518 fires. Those 372 contain 131 winners worth +$18,483 we forgo and 241 losers worth −$18,115 we avoid. Net effect of the entire filter on the bottom line: +$368.

So the ATR band does not find money. It concentrates the same money into a quarter of the trades. That is genuinely worth something — it is what lifts $7.46 a trade to $23.95 and what makes the thing survive strip-best-10 — but anyone reading “+0.9R a trade” as “the filter found an edge” has it backwards. The filter found a smaller sample. And the band edges were chosen after looking at the data: there were ~36 plausible lo×hi combinations, so a p of 0.029 corrected for that multiplicity looks like a p of about 1.0.

Fourteen dollars, over eleven months. Month by month it is a coin toss — +734, −84, +437, −1,510, −1,333, −555, +373, +496, +1,844, −669, +281 — six green months of eleven. It does not survive stripping its three best trades. The ATR band is not useless: without it the same entries lose $3,640. The band takes the rider from clearly negative to exactly zero. That is a real filter doing real work on a mechanism with no edge to protect.

One tick of slippage takes it to −$1,275. And the power calculation: at the band midpoint 1R is $25 and the round trip costs 0.060R; to establish a real expectancy of +0.10R against what was actually measured needs roughly 10,500 trades, about eight years of this rider's fire rate. This is not a “needs more data” case. It is a “the effect is smaller than the measurement error and smaller than the costs” case.

3.7 Why the mechanism fails, in one paragraph

The arithmetic that closes the case

At the moment the boarding rule is true, the median run has 12 to 41 points left depending on how much confirmation you demand — and the same rule is true 34 times a day on tape that does nothing. The trades that are not runs pay the stop, which at 3×ATR in the 11–16 band is 33 to 48 points. So the rider risks 33–48 points to collect a median 12–41, and roughly one fire in twenty is a run. The wide exit helps because it lets the occasional big run pay for many stops, but the ratio at the moment of firing is simply too thin. Boarding is solved. Selecting is not.

3.8 The one thing that goes forward, and the router rule that keeps it dark

Splitting the 1,289 out-of-sample boards by session, the overnight leg is the only survivor: +$4,406 on 383 trades, +0.485R, against Europe −$6.00/tr and the US −$4.14/tr. On its own generating sample it survives strip-best-10, leave-one-day-out, leave-one-month-out and a full point of slippage each side. Three small code changes are required first, and shipping around any of them records a different strategy under this name — which is exactly how rider_w5 came to be quoted at +$19.29 while making −$28.84. (1) gate_board cannot express a wrapping clock window — it tests hh_lo <= h < hh_hi, never true for 21:00→07:00. (2) ShadowVariant has an atr_max but no atr_min; the rule is a band and an arm with only the ceiling is a different arm. (3) ShadowVariant.er_min reads ER30, and substituting ER30 flips this rule's sign on the held-out sample — the arm must gate on the 10-minute ER. Also: adverse_cut_atr=99 on purpose, because rider_w5's tight give-back cut fired on 35% of its live trades for −$1,449 and is the exact opposite of the wide-exit thesis this arm exists to test.

Promotion bar, deliberately set high — all four, not P&L alone: 200 shadow trades including at least one genuinely trending overnight week; ≥ +0.15R after stripping its best three; the daytime control arm must be materially worse; and the counter-trend control must stay negative. If that control reverses again, the arm is measuring noise and should be retired without argument.

4 PER CLUSTER

Four separate labs, one per cluster, each with its own harness and its own out-of-sample legs. Each one was told to interrogate the label before trusting anything built on top of it. All four found the label was the story.

UNCLASS — 41 runs, $5,840, 61% of the money  LABEL REFUTED RIDER PARKED

The verdict: the rider was built, ridden, filtered and routed — and it still lost. It boards 40 of the 41 runs at a median 3.3 minutes late with 2.57 ATRs still ahead, so detection is fine here too.

What killed it: the placebo/shuffle test, on two independent samples. On 205 untouched sessions the real signal makes +$3.54 a trade and the same rule taken thirty minutes early makes +$4.44. On the 11-session forward leg the real signal makes −$9.58 and the fake makes +$1.48. Ten trades out of 1,246 carry the entire result. And the family kill is stronger than the single cell: across 137 configurations with usable n on both independent legs, 13% are positive on both against a 12% chance rate — no setting of this rider replicates any better than a coin.

The label finding, which outranks it: UNCLASS is true on 73.9% of the census week and 75.4% of the whole lake, takes 60.3% of the runs, lift 0.82. Its members sit on the all-tape median for ATR (11.65 vs 12.31), volume (1.06 vs 1.02) and efficiency (0.153 vs 0.163), and their direction is a 22/19 coin flip. Hunting a footprint inside UNCLASS is a category error — the other three branches are the tests that find footprints, so the residue is by construction the runs with none. And 21 of the 68 cluster labels flip on nothing but where a sixty-second bucket starts; only 17 of 68 run timestamps are minute-aligned. Rename it UNTESTED and report it as coverage.

The prize restated: $5,840 headline → $3,239 (55%) to a cheating oracle → $1,592 (27%) to an honest late board with a real stop. Two thirds of the number on the census page cannot be collected by anything entering after the move has proven itself.

Two things go forward, and both are router instruments rather than gates: BENCH 07:00–13:00Z for any late-boarding rider — red on all three legs (−$29.38 / −$20.04 / −$2.43, the last on n=373), the only piece of session structure that replicates; and shadow the ER30 arm-meter with its own control arm — +$3.54 vs −$3.40 a trade on 3,238 untouched trades, the largest-n effect in the section, but it reverses on the recent eleven sessions and that reversal is the cheapest open question in the bucket. Also refuted: last week's shadow pick UNCL-RIDER-ASIA was never actually shipped — no arm of that name exists in shadow.db — and replayed forward it is four consecutive red weeks (−$2.93, −$15.90, −$36.46, −$38.08), with the pure forward tape since 08-17 at −$472 over 16 trades. It also boards only 3 of the week's 41 runs and was never an answer to this bucket.

OPEN/NEWS — 10 runs, $1,768  NULL — nothing ships

Nine gates built, nine graves — ORB30, COILBRK, PULSE, PULSE-13h, FADE1, RNGX60, VWRC, OAB, MOMBRK, BOOKLEAN. Best on paper was MOMBRK at +$32.87 a trade over 52 trades.

What killed the whole section: a gate with no trigger at all beats every gate built. “Enter at 13:05Z every day, direction = sign of the last 15 minutes, stop 0.5×U, hold 30 minutes” — no signal, no arm, no condition — books +$47.29 a trade over 26 trades. That is double PULSE-13h (+$23.09) and 44% more than MOMBRK. The identical trigger-free rule 25 minutes later books −$13.46. Every candidate on the page lives inside the noise band of a control containing no information. The confidence intervals say the same thing: t = 1.30, day-block bootstrap 95% CI [−$4.14, +$26.27], and 561 trades ≈ 79 trading days needed to resolve a $10/trade edge in a two-hour window. We have 27.

Individually: MOMBRK died on strip-the-best (top 3 of 52 trades are 88.5% of the net; strip-3 → +$4.02, strip-5 → −$11.37) plus an OOS leg of −$16.50. PULSE-13h died on the stale placebo — a copy of the signal delayed 30 minutes books +$23.95, more than the real signal's +$23.09.

The label: the 08-15 fix correctly demoted OPEN/NEWS from checked-first to checked-last, taking it from 100% to 58.7% of its own window. But its lift over the bare clock is 0.921× — the two footprint tests strip out 41% of the window and keep the less run-dense half. Deleting them would make the bucket a better predictor. Meanwhile VOL-EXPANSION, the label the census barely uses, carries 4.18× lift on 1.95% of tape — the sharpest cluster the census produces and the one it uses least.

The deliverable is the size threshold. Bucketed by realised 30-minute follow-through: −20 to +20pt → −$38.44 a trade; +60 to +120pt → −$4.10; ≥+120pt → +$184.75. A move of sixty to a hundred and twenty points in your favour still loses money. Break-even hit rate is 19.2% and the arm delivered 30.8% — that 11.6-point margin is the entire edge, and it does not survive strip-3.   • No rider goes live, and the one that goes to shadow goes as a question with a four-part promotion bar, not as a candidate. Its held-out strip-best-3 is −$51 — three trades were the whole thing. It is not a number to size on.
  • Nothing from any of the four clusters ships, not even to shadow. OPEN/NEWS explicitly: nothing, because a gate with no trigger in it beat everything built.
  • No gold cell is promoted until the bar source is fixed. Until then the lab and the desk are running different gates.
  • Thin n means SHADOW, never a live promotion. Every number on this page that looks good is either in-sample, or carried by a handful of trades, or both — and the two arms this desk did promote to shadow off last Friday's version of this work are both red and both retired today. That is the pattern to break, and the way to break it is the promotion bar, not the enthusiasm.

7.5 If we still cannot catch them — what the next attempt actually needs

Plainly: we cannot catch them, and more days will not fix it. The power calculation says ~10,500 trades — about eight years of this rider's fire rate — to separate the measured effect from zero. The out-of-sample legs are already 236 sessions and 205 sessions. This is not a sample-size problem for the rider; it is a missing-instrument problem.

The one sentence to take away

Boarding is solved — a five-minute trigger gets inside 62 of 62 runs with 41 points still ahead. Selecting is not — the same trigger fires 34 times a day and about one fire in twenty is a run. The honest prize is $2,000–$5,000 a week, not $9,610, and today we convert none of it. Meanwhile the two runs we were positioned against cost −$3,438, and the whipsaw bench in §7.2 addresses a measured −$1,383.50. The money on this page is not in catching the runs we missed. It is in not fighting the ones we find.
