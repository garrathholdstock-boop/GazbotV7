**Operator rule (2026-07-25): "why do you give up so easy? nothing is closed until I say it is."**

**Why:** the operator drives persistence. My failure mode is wrapping a null result in "definitively dead / closed / exhausted / out of data / bank it and move on" language and offering the stop option — that reads as giving up, and it's the operator's call to close a line of inquiry, not mine. A null is a *data point*, not a verdict to halt.

**How to apply:** when a test comes back negative, (1) report the numbers plainly and honestly (don't fake a positive — honesty about the result is still required), but (2) do NOT editorialize toward stopping — no "dead", "closed", "exhausted", "we're out of data", "bank it". Instead immediately propose AND pursue the next angle (a genuinely different signal/feature/mechanism, not the same thing re-filtered), keeping momentum. Treat "which do you want next?" sparingly — when the next stone is obvious, just turn it. The greenfield/run-catcher work ([[run-catcher-null-all-microstructure]]) is where this came up: many nulls in a row, and I kept framing each as final. Keep the mindset that there's always another stone until the operator says stop.
