**07-30 (operator caught it live):** in the afternoon roll-over CHOP (ER 0.01–0.08), I flipped the book back to momentum longs (grind+abs_veto_long) on a 4-bucket buyer-delta build (+776→+1601) that broke the afternoon range-top 28080→28125. It FADED within 2 minutes (sellers −1087, price back to 28068), ER stayed 0.01. grind fired 2 longs into the chop and went underwater. Operator: "why is grind buying into chop. bench it." — correct.

**Why:** a delta-build + a marginal (+45pt) range-top poke in a deep-chop tape is just the UPPER SWING of the oscillation, not a trend resumption. Aggressor delta is a LEADING signal but on its own it is too eager in chop — buyers can aggress and price still round-trips (absorption). The real morning breakouts had **ER 0.40 + a decisive sustained move**; this had **ER 0.01 + an immediate stall**.

**How to apply:** to re-arm momentum coming OUT of chop, require the EFFICIENCY to confirm, not just delta: price breaks the band AND **ER actually climbs** (e.g. last-hr ER rising through ~0.20+ toward trend), AND the move sustains for more than one bounce. A buyer/seller delta-build alone, or a few-point range-top poke while ER is still ~0, is NOT enough — it's the oscillation, keep the fade/reversion book. This is the mirror-caution to [[us-open-dont-bench-trend-rider]]: that one says don't BENCH a trend-rider on chop-noise; this one says don't ARM a momentum gate on chop-noise. Both: judge the REGIME (ER + sustain), not a single flow blip. Related: [[exhaustion-short-live-fader-success]] (the fade book that belongs in the roll-over chop), [[router-benching-lessons-0729]].
