Operator 2026-07-08 spotted it: "the losses on the sim never seem as vicious as the live desk." **Confirmed, structural.** The shadow's reversion exit (`exit_desk`) and the tick reprice (`replay_exit`) model the stop as a **clean fill at the exact 1-ATR stop price** — and `grep force_kill|slippage` across `shadow_strategies.py` + `passive_through_fill_backtest.py` = **0 matches**. So the shadow:
- assumes a PERFECT 1-ATR stop fill (no slippage),
- has NO `FORCE_KILL` (the live desk's runaway-cutter),
- caps every loss at ~1 ATR.

The live desk does NOT — the 1-ATR stop is a native trail that lags/slips on a fast adverse move, and FORCE_KILL catches the runaway at a much bigger loss. **Proof (2026-07-08, 7 live force-kills):** every one bigger than a clean 1-ATR stop — MGC −$65 vs ~$21 (3.1×), MNQ −$48.5 vs ~$31 (1.6×), MES −$16 vs ~$10 (1.6×); avg ~1.6×, worst 3.1×. The shadow would have shown that day's fade book at ~HALF the real loss.

**How to apply:** the shadow's WINNERS are honest (tick-repriced), but its LOSERS are too gentle → **every shadow net-P&L is biased OPTIMISTIC by the understated tail**, worst where the live desk hurts most (fades run over on a trend). When reading shadow numbers, treat losses as a FLOOR and mentally haircut the net — especially for reversion/fade sims and on trend days. **FIX (Friday scope, not armed):** in `replay_exit`, fill the stop at the ACTUAL adverse touch when the tape blows past it (slippage), and add the FORCE_KILL backstop so runaways cut where live cuts. Until then, do NOT quote a shadow loss as the real downside. See [[sims-on-tick-price]], [[shadow-momentum-chandelier]].
